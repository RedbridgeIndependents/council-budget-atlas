Agenda item

Budget Framework 2026/27 and Medium Term Financial Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31

  • Meeting of Full Council, Wednesday, 25 February 2026 7:00 pm (Item 61.)
  • View the background to item 61.

Minutes:

The Leader introduced the Council’s proposed Budget Framework 2026/27 and Medium Term Financial Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31.

 

In presenting the report, the Leader highlighted the recent achievements of the borough against a background of over 14 years of financial austerity from the previous Conservative Government, which included:

 

-  The recent royal visit to Barking & Dagenham by Their Majesties King Charles III & Queen Camilla;

-  Youth Justice Services rated as ‘Good’ following a full inspection by His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Probation;

-  A world class dental training school secured that would improve general health outcomes for children and adults alike; and

-  An intellectual partnership with Queen Mary University of London agreed which was raising the quality and ambition of the Councils neighbourhood services.

 

Despite the ongoing pressures facing local government across the country, we have achieved a balanced General Fund budget for 2026/27. This has only been possible through a combination of disciplined financial planning and targeted investment. 

 

The Leader advised that £8.7 million in savings had been identified, including £1.8 million of invest?to?save initiatives which would reduce costs over the longer term. Alongside this, the budget included £32.8 million of growth to protect services and meet rising demand.  Crucially, the introduction of the new Local Government Fair Funding Formula strengthened the financial position for the coming year, recognising the pressures and needs within the borough. 

 

Due to the multi-year financial settlement covering 2026/27 to 2028/29, the first three years of the financial plan were stabilised. To address longer term structural pressures, the Council launched a Change Programme aimed at delivering £50 million in savings over the next three years. 

 

To maintain essential services, a 4.99% Council Tax increase was proposed, which included the Adult Social Care precept.   For a Band D property this meant an annual charge of £1,687.99, an increase of £80.23, which was equivalent to £1.54 a week. The GLA precept was due to rise to £510.51, an increase of 4.1%. 


The Leader reflected on three key areas which defined financial challenges for the Council moving forward: 

 

·  Demand continued to increase in both adults’ and children’s social care.  There was a shift in demand from older people to working?age adults with complex needs, which placed significant pressure on budgets.  Children’s residential placements had seen substantial cost increases and show no signs of slowing;

·  While Consumer Prices Index inflation had stabilised, it remained high enough to affect the cost of the contracts the Council relied on.  The Retail Prices Index, which was still used for many contracts, remained elevated.  Construction inflation and high interest rates continue to impact the Investment & Acquisition Strategy, reducing the viability of schemes that were previously forecast to generate income; and

·  Homelessness pressures remained a significant risk area.

 

The Council’s total borrowing stood at £1.6 billion, of which £1.1 billion related to the IAS.  Given recent economic conditions, rising interest rates, and increased construction costs, new construction schemes funded through the IAS had been paused which was the prudent and responsible choice.

 

The General Fund reserve remained at £12 million, consistent with policy.  The Section 25 Statement highlighted several key risks that the Council must navigate carefully: social care volatility, IAS exposure, inflation, subsidiary performance, borrowing affordability, and temporary accommodation pressures. 

 

In response to questions, the Leader advised that the Council had in place discretionary support for its most vulnerable residents, totalling over £5m of support for residents.  The Council would continue to support those families who needed it.  Without including the Adult Social Care Precept (which amounted to a 2% rise in Council Tax Bad D equivalent) the Council would not be able to deliver a balanced budget and would not be able to continue to financially support and open up opportunities to the most vulnerable families in the borough.

 

Furthermore, the Leader advised that investments made by the Council provided returns to the borough which had protected frontline services at times where other Local Authorities had made significant service cuts.  The investments had also enabled local residents to move into good quality, new and affordable accommodation.

 

In accordance with paragraph 10.3.2 of Part 2, Chapter 3 of the Council Constitution, the budget was put to a recorded vote and was agreed as follows:

 

For: Councillors Akwaboah, Ashraf, P Bright, S Bright, Channer, Chowdhury, Dulwich, Fergus, Freeborn, Geddes, Ghani, Haroon, Hussain, Jones, Kangethe, Khan, Lee, Masson, Miah, Oluwole, Perry, Pongo, Rahman, Rai, P Robinson, Saleem, Sandhu, Shaukat, Shaw, Siddiqui, Sohaib, Spoor, Twomey, Williams and Worby (35)

 

Against: Councillors Choudhury, Hornby, Quadri and L Rice (4)

 

Abstain: None (0)

 

The Assembly resolved to:

 

(i)  Agree that the amount of Council Tax (Band D equivalent) shall increase by 4.99%, inclusive of the Adult Social Care Precept of 2%;

 

(ii)  Agree that the Council Tax to be set for 2026/27 shall be £1,687.99 for a Band D property, an annual increase of £80.23;

 

(iii)  Note that the Council shall levy an additional £510.51 on the Band D amount above on behalf of the Greater London Authority, which represented an increase of 4.1%;

 

(iv)  Note the amount of 57,526.52 as the Council Tax Base for Barking and Dagenham for 2026/27, a decrease of 620.28 on the previous year, in accordance with Regulation 3 of the Local Authorities (Calculation of Council Tax Base) Regulations 1992 made under the Local Government Finance Act 1992 (as amended);

 

(v)  Agree, in setting the Council’s General Fund revenue budget, to set the Council Tax requirement at £97.1m for 2026/27;

 

(vi)  Consider and have due regard to budget consultation feedback with residents and businesses as set out in Section 20 of the report;

 

(vii)  Agree the Statutory Budget Determination for 2026/27 as set out at Appendix D to the report;

 

(viii)  Approve the proposed General Fund Revenue Budget for 2026/27 at £265.755m as set out in Appendix A to the report;

 

(ix)  Agree the new savings, including Invest-to-Save, and growth proposals as set out in Appendix B to the report;

 

(x)  Approve the latest General Fund Medium Term Financial Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31 as set out in section 3 and Appendix A to the report;

 

(xi)  Note that the proposals to maintain a General Fund balance of £12m in line with the Council’s approved Reserves Policy (July 2023);

 

(xii)  Note the projected reserve balances at 31 March 2026, as set out in section 19 of the report;

 

(xiii)  Approve the Council’s provisional Capital Programme, including Investment and Acquisition Strategy (IAS) schemes, for 2026/27 to 2027/28 as detailed section 17 and Appendix G to the report;

 

(xiv)  Approve the Council’s updated Capital Budget for 2025/26, as set out in section 17 of the report;

 

(xv)  Agree to set a Capital Budget for 2026/27 at £126.7m, as detailed in Appendix G to the report;

 

(xvi)  Approve the Strategy for the Flexible Use of Capital Receipts at Appendix I to the report, in line with the regulatory requirements to facilitate the delivery of efficiency savings including capitalisation of redundancy costs;

 

(xvii) Note and approve the revisions between the Provisional and Final Local Government Finance Settlement, as set out in Section 21 of the report; and

 

(xviii) Note the Chief Financial Officer’s Statutory Finance Report (Section 25 Statement) as set out in Appendix H to the report and, in particular, their determination of “the robustness of the estimates made for the purposes of the calculations, and the adequacy of the proposed financial reserves”.
