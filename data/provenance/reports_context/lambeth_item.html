Agenda item

Revenue and Capital Budget 2026/27

  • Meeting of Budget Council, Council, Wednesday 4 March 2026 7.00 pm (Item 5.)

Minutes:

Councillor Judith Cavanagh, Cabinet Member for Finance (Acting), introduced the Revenue and Capital Budget 2026/27 and highlighted that:

  • The budget placed the Council on a path toward financial resilience and stability following fourteen years of reduced local government funding and reflected Lambeth’s values of equity and justice while prioritising support for residents.
  • Thanks were expressed to Cabinet Members, officers and the finance team for their work in preparing the budget.
  • Improvements across a range of services were highlighted, including youth justice, schools, parks, libraries and youth provision, with the Youth Justice Service recently rated “good” with outstanding features and Lambeth schools overwhelmingly rated good or outstanding.
  • Investment and achievements across the borough were noted, including one of the largest School Streets programmes in London, free swimming for children under 11, and 26 Green Flag awards for parks and open spaces.
  • Support for vulnerable residents was highlighted, including £20 million in cost-of-living support assisting more than 40,000 households, free school meal holiday provision for around 18,000 families, and over £100 million spent on Council Tax Support alongside outreach services for people experiencing homelessness.
  • Investment proposals within the budget included approximately £250 million for adult social care and health, £130 million for children and families’ services, £98 million for housing and homelessness services, and £52 million for council housing.
  • The budget was developed in the context of significant financial pressures and included major investment in key services, including adult social care and health, children and families’ services, housing and homelessness support, council housing, community safety initiatives and neighbourhood improvements, with the aim of ensuring Lambeth remained a borough where residents could thrive.

 

Councillor Nicole Griffiths proposed the Green Group amendment to the budget and highlighted that:

  • The administration’s budget proposed nearly £100 million in cuts and savings alongside £150 million in exceptional government borrowing, which raised concerns that these reductions would have a significant negative impact on vulnerable and low-income residents.
  • Concern was expressed that the proposed budget would remove essential support in a borough with high levels of poverty, with over one third of children living in poverty, and that reduced services and insufficient funding could exacerbate inequality and increase future demand for services.
  • Criticism was raised that the administration’s approach to housing and financial management had contributed to pressures linked to Homes for Lambeth, including impacts on the Housing Revenue Account, the sale of council homes and assets, and longstanding estate maintenance issues.
  • Further criticism was raised regarding unrealistic budget setting, weaknesses in contract management and governance, and rising legal costs associated with housing repairs and social housing management.
  • The proposed amendment sought to protect vulnerable residents by redistributing funding towards frontline services, including council tax support, anti-poverty initiatives and housing provision alongside generating revenue through increased fees on higher-polluting vehicles and utilising developer contributions to support services and improve air quality.
  • It was stated that the Green Group supported an anti-austerity approach focused on investment in people and public services, advocating for greater wealth redistribution, increased council housing provision and fully funded health and social care, alongside a fairer tax system including taxation of wealth to support a more equitable society.

 

Councillor Ben Curtis  proposed the Liberal Democrat Group amendment and highlighted the following:

  • Concern was raised that Lambeth’s financial position was extremely challenging, with reserves reportedly around £5 million, reliance on exceptional government support to balance the budget, a projected £129 million funding gap by 2029/30, and council tax increasing by the maximum permitted level.
  • The amendment proposed achieving savings through greater efficiency, including sharing back-office functions with neighbouring boroughs, with a 2% reduction in administrative costs estimated to save approximately £1.147 million by 2027/28.
  • The proposals built on existing shared service arrangements, including counter-fraud services, with the intention of extending this approach to additional areas.
  • A modest late-night levy on licensed premises was proposed to generate revenue for community safety patrols and enhanced street cleaning in night-time economy areas.
  • Additional savings proposals included reducing special responsibility allowances for certain council roles following restructuring and introducing a charging system for impounded e-bikes to improve pavement accessibility.
  • Further measures included increasing fly-tipping penalties to generate additional funding for enforcement, with the amendment presented as an alternative approach intended to deliver a budget within the Council’s means while prioritising services for residents.
