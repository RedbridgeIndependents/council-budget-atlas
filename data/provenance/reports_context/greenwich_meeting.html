Decision:

Noted that there is increased pressure on the finances of the Housing Revenue Account (HRA) in 2026/27 due to the cost of repairs, increased requirements for regulatory compliance, and the impact of inflation. See section 4.2 below for more details on these pressures and the risks that they pose to the HRA.

 

Noted that a HRA Voluntary Revenue Provision on amounts above the Major Repairs Reserve will not be provided in 2026/27, to assist the HRA in achieving a balanced budget and adequate Minimum Working Balance. This aligns with the Treasury Management and Capital Strategy, with further detail contained in the Minimum Revenue Provision Policy.

 

Agreed to the following changes to rents and charges which partially mitigate the financial pressures on the HRA:

 

·        To follow the national rent policy, as set out in the Rent Standard, and to approve an increase in rents for council homes by 4.8% (September's CPI + 1%), the maximum allowed by government. This will increase the average weekly rent to £118.35.

·        To note that the government made an announcement in relation to rent convergence on 28 January 2025 (see section 4.3.2):

o   to commence from 2027/28 at a rate of £1 per week (included in the calculations within this report)

o   increasing to £2 per week from 2028/29 (not included in the calculations within this report, but which provisionally could provide a further £0.850m of rental income from that year and further sums beyond).

·        To agree an increase in Tenant Service Charges for council homes by 5.3%, resulting in an increase of 93p per week, according to the methodology set out in section 4.4.3 below. This will increase the service charge to £18.65.

·        To agree to increase rents for tenants in HRA and General Fund temporary accommodation by 3.8% (CPI) to an average of £310.55 for a 2 bed flat.

·        To agree to increase garage rents by £1 a week, between 6.5% and 8.4% depending on garage tier.

·        To agree to increase parking charges on estates by £3 per year for an annual permit, an increase of 5.2%, and by 50p per 10 visitor permits an increase of 14.3%.

 

Noted that even with these proposed increases, the HRA will still be under significant financial pressure.

 

Agreed to the following proposals, which address the financial pressures on the HRA, described in detail in section 4.6:

 

·        To moderate the amount spent on legal disrepair cases (where tenants take legal action against the council for issues in their homes) by intervening earlier during cases.

·        To reduce spend on voids works (maintenance to homes before new tenants move in) through improved project management and contract management.

·        To improve rent collection levels and reduce arrears rates for tenants in HRA and General Fund temporary accommodation by improving processes and maximising use of technology.

·        To resolve technical issues with incorrect energy bills that have been paid by the HRA.

Minutes:

Councillor Pat Slattery, Cabinet Member for Housing Management, Neighbourhoods and Homelessness introduced the Report. She said it should be noted that the HRA was a ring?fenced account which was separate from Council Tax accounts and could not be subsidised from these accounts.  It was fully funded through tenant rents and leaseholder service charges and rents for 2026/27 would be set in accordance with the national formula.  Whilst price increases were never welcomed, it was a requirement that the HRA was balanced and maintained adequate reserves.  Even with the increase, the Council continued to provide the second lowest council rents in London. The Hardship Fund would continue and there was evidence in the report that it was helping those who needed it.

 

She updated Members on cost pressures within the HRA, including the requirement to achieve full compliance with building safety regulations and to implement new statutory duties.  She highlighted the performance of the Council’s award winning damp and mould team and confirmed that the Regulator of Social Housing had lifted the compliance notice originally issued following the Council’s self referral in May 2022.  Other efficiencies to note included the renegotiation of terms and conditions for repairs staff, including the removal of a legacy bonus scheme, which had resulted in substantial and ongoing salary savings, projected to deliver hundreds of thousands of pounds per year over the medium term.

 

Councillor Charlie Davis, Deputy Leader of the Conservative Group proposed an amendment as published.  He thanked officers for the progress made with temporary accommodation and the conversion of voids and improved management strategy.  Their amendment proposed a formal review of communication with tenants and leaseholders that went beyond the statutory minimum when they were facing significant bills. They sought to establish a fund to assist leaseholders who had been impacted by Capital Work charges from the establishment of a modest, capped fund. Their amendment also sought to strengthen the Council’s approach to anti-social behaviour from council tenants.

 

The amendment was seconded by Councillor Matt Hartley, Leader of the Conservative Group who said that they were proposing to extend the scope of the Hardship Fund given the rent rises which many residents would struggle with.

 

Councillor Pat Slattery, Cabinet Member for Housing Management, Neighbourhoods and Homelessness did not accept the amendment.

 

Councillor Lakshan Saldin from the Independent and Green Group rose to speak on the amendment.  He thanked officers for their work and said that whilst there was a lot they agreed with in the amendment, such as limited aid to leaseholders facing financial hardship, and that the right to social housing came with conditions regarding behaviour, they found some of the wording problematic.  They believed that social housing was a right rather than a privilege and so they were not able to support the amendment and would be abstaining from voting on the amendment on that basis.
