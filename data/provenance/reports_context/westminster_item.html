Agenda item

Business and Financial Planning 2026/27 to 2028/29

  • Meeting of Cabinet, Monday 23rd February, 2026 6.30 pm (Item 4.)
  • View the background to item 4.

To consider the annual budget report (which sets out the recommendation for Council Tax setting for 2026/27), the overall budget position for 2026/27 and estimates underpinning financial planning over the medium-term.

Decision:

That Cabinet approves the following recommendations to Full Council for consideration at its meeting on 4 March 2026:

 

Council Tax

 

·       That the Council Tax for a Band D be agreed at £537.34 for 2026/27.

·       That subject to the consideration of the previous recommendation, the council tax for the City of Westminster, excluding the Montpelier Square area and Queen’s Park Community Council, for the year ending 31 March 2027, be as specified in the Council Tax Resolution in Appendix 1.

·       That the Precepts and Special Expenses be as also specified in Appendix 1 for properties in Montpelier Square and the Queen’s Park Community Council

·       That the formal resolution for 2026/27 attached at Appendix 1 including the council tax requirement of £83.214m be agreed.

·       Note the proposed Greater London Authority precept (Band D) of £510.51, an increase of £20.13 (4.1%) in the adjusted Band D precept.

·       That the Council continues the Westminster Community Contribution scheme to allow residents in the City to voluntarily contribute towards supporting discretionary services that support the four priorities of: helping young people reach their full potential, including staying safe; contributing positively to their community and developing longer term skills; providing extra support for people who find themselves sleeping on Westminster’s streets; tackling social isolation and loneliness; and promoting social inclusion.

 

Revenue Budget

 

·       To note the views of the Scrutiny Budget Task Group set out in Appendix 2.

·       That the proposed General Fund net budget requirement of £282.666m summarised in Appendix 3 is approved.

·       That £9.84m of the general fund balance and £9.84m of earmarked reserves are used to partially fund the budget in 2026/27.

·       That the savings, pressures and investments for 2026/27 to 2028/29 set out in Appendix 4, 5 and 6, are approved.

·       That the Equality Impact Assessments included in Appendix 7 are noted to inform the consideration of the budget.

·       To note the Housing Revenue Account (HRA) Business Plan 2026/27 and 30 Year Housing Investment Plan presented concurrently to Cabinet on 23 February 2026 that recommends the HRA budget and rent levels for 2026/27.

·       To note the Dedicated Schools Grant (DSG) allocations totalling £210.162m and agree that the schools block of £139.144m is allocated to schools using the local formula agreed by Westminster Schools Forum which reflects the majority of the National Funding Formula factor values, with the pupil led funding being higher to reflect the overall Schools Block allocation and meets DfE requirements.

·       That the Executive Director of Finance and Resources be delegated responsibility for any technical adjustments required to be made to the budget.

·       That the cost of inflation, pressures and contingencies be issued to service budgets if the need materialises, to the limits contained within the budget calculations.

·       That the views of consultees and consultation approach, as set out in the relevant section be considered by Council.

 

Capital Programme

 

·       To note the Capital Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31, forecast position for 2025/26 and future years’ forecasts summarised up to 2039/40 that sets out the Council’s capital programme and financing summarised in this report and set out in detail in a concurrent report on this agenda.

 

Reserves, Balances and Budget Estimates

 

·       To agree the reserves policy as set out in section 12.

·       To note the report of the Section 151 Officer regarding the robustness of the budget process, the estimates underpinning the budget and the adequacy of the reserves as set out in Appendix 8 as required by S25 of the Local Government Act 2003.

 

Treasury Management and Investment Framework

 

·       To note the Treasury Management Strategy for 2026/27 including the annual investment strategy, borrowing limits and prudential indicators summarised in this report and set out in detail in a concurrent report on this agenda.

·       To note the 2026/27 Integrated Investment Framework report also concurrently on this agenda, which sets out the policies and framework for future investment decisions for the Council.

 

Minutes:

Councillor Boothroyd (Cabinet Member for Finance and Council Reform) introduced the revenue budget, and set out how it would contribute to a Fairer Westminster including through cost-of-living support, a reorganised homelessness service, a full review of the City Plan, mental health beds following the Gordon Hospital closure, the CCTV network and community hubs. He noted that £54 million in new savings had been identified to fund the investments and cope with pressures like temporary accommodation and adult social care, and thanked the Cabinet Members, officers and councillors on the Budget Scrutiny Task Group who contributed to the budget process.

 

Councillor Boothroyd highlighted the difficult background to the budget due to the Fair Funding Review and that the Council has embarked on a whole council transformation plan to deliver substantial recurring baseline savings in future years and is working with the GLA and Government to design an overnight visitor levy scheme to ensure long-term sustainability. In light of expected long-term savings and long-term income, he said the Council can reasonably transfer £19.6 million funding from reserves, which have been built up for that purpose, and freeze the Westminster City Council element of Council Tax and allow the social care contribution to rise by below the rate of inflation.

 

Councillor Jason Williams (Chair of the Budget Scrutiny Task Group) noted that the group had rigorously challenged the budget and recognised the financial challenges, identifying homelessness and temporary accommodation as major ongoing risks and stating that he considered the proposals robust.

 

Gerald Almeroth (Executive Director of Finance and Resources) set out further figures including the £20 million reduction in core grant as a result of the Fair Funding Review for 2026/27, highlighting the challenges with the projected £88 million budget gap by the end of the three-year period despite the £100 million of planned savings in that period.

 

Councillor Butler-Thalassis (Deputy Leader and Cabinet Member for Adult Social Care, Public Health and Voluntary Sector) thanked the adult social care team for their savings and highlighted the pressures on them due to an ageing population and the pandemic's lingering effects, stressing the importance of the ring-fenced social care precept.

 

Councillor Ormsby (Cabinet Member for Regeneration and Renters) praised the decision to freeze the main element of Council Tax to ensure an average band D property sees a rise of 20p per week while being able to deliver on top priorities.

 

Councillor Hug thanked those involved in putting together the budget in a difficult financial context, expressing confidence in the transformation work and highlighting the potential for an overnight visitor levy to generate approximately £49 million for Westminster and the work ongoing to ensure the highest possible share of this is retained. Councillor Barraclough (Cabinet Member for Planning and Economic Development) added that while Westminster businesses pay £2.7 billion in business rates, only £53 million is retained locally, justifying a significant share of any new levy.

 

RESOLVED: That Cabinet agreed to the recommendations as set out on pages 13 - 14 of the Private Report Pack.
