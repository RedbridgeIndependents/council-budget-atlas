Minutes:

The Leader introduced the Council’s proposed Budget Framework 2026/27 and Medium Term Financial Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31.

 

In presenting the report, the Leader highlighted the recent achievements of the borough against a background of over 14 years of financial austerity from the previous Conservative Government, which included:

 

-  The recent royal visit to Barking & Dagenham by Their Majesties King Charles III & Queen Camilla;

-  Youth Justice Services rated as ‘Good’ following a full inspection by His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Probation;

-  A world class dental training school secured that would improve general health outcomes for children and adults alike; and

-  An intellectual partnership with Queen Mary University of London agreed which was raising the quality and ambition of the Councils neighbourhood services.

 

Despite the ongoing pressures facing local government across the country, we have achieved a balanced General Fund budget for 2026/27. This has only been possible through a combination of disciplined financial planning and targeted investment. 

 

The Leader advised that £8.7 million in savings had been identified, including £1.8 million of invest?to?save initiatives which would reduce costs over the longer term. Alongside this, the budget included £32.8 million of growth to protect services and meet rising demand.  Crucially, the introduction of the new Local Government Fair Funding Formula strengthened the financial position for the coming year, recognising the pressures and needs within the borough. 

 

Due to the multi-year financial settlement covering 2026/27 to 2028/29, the first three years of the financial plan were stabilised. To address longer term structural pressures, the Council launched a Change Programme aimed at delivering £50 million in savings over the next three years. 

 

To maintain essential services, a 4.99% Council Tax increase was proposed, which included the Adult Social Care precept.   For a Band D property this meant an annual charge of £1,687.99, an increase of £80.23, which was equivalent to £1.54 a week. The GLA precept was due to rise to £510.51, an increase of 4.1%. 


The Leader reflected on three key areas which defined financial challenges for the Council moving forward: 

 

·  Demand continued to increase in both adults’ and children’s social care.  There was a shift in demand from older people to working?age adults with complex needs, which placed significant pressure on budgets.  Children’s residential placements had seen substantial cost increases and show no signs of slowing;

·  Homelessness pressures remained a significant risk area.

Minutes:

The Leader of the Council introduced the Treasury Management Strategy Statement (TMSS) 2026/27 and Capital Strategy 2026/27 to 2027/28 report which set out the Council’s borrowing, investment and funding plans for the year ahead. The Capital Strategy set out an overview of how the Council’s capital investment contributed to the provision of local public services and considered the financing of that and whether it was prudent, affordable and sustainable.

 

The Cabinet Member highlighted the main points within the report, which had been recommended by the Cabinet at its meeting on 16 February 2026.

 

In response to questions, the Leader advised that the Council had in place a long-term treasury management strategy, which included mitigations and buffers to deal with various scenarios which may impact on the councils borrowing.  The vast majority of the Councils borrowing was locked in on long term rates, with short term borrowing the Council would review potential changes in rates and would adjust its strategy accordingly.  Furthermore, the Leader advised there would be no cuts to frontline services if rates were to increase as the strategy planned for such scenarios.

 

The Assembly resolved to approve and adopt the Treasury Management Strategy Statement for 2026/27 and, in doing so, to:

 

(i)  Approve the Capital Strategy, as set out at Appendix 1 to the report, and the consequent borrowing forecasts for the period 2026/27 to 2027/28;

 

(ii)  Approve the Capital Prudential and Treasury Indicators 2026/27 to 2028/29, as set out at Appendix 2 to the report;

 

(iii)  Note the strategic approach to future borrowing as set out in section 5 of the report;

 

(iv)  Approve the Treasury Investment Strategy, as set out in section 6 of the report, including an unlimited limit for deposits with the Debt Management Account Deposit Facility;

(vi)  Approve the revised Minimum Revenue Provision Policy Statement 2026/27 as set out at Appendix 3 to the report; and

 

(vii)  Note the risks set out in section 12 of the report.

Minutes:

The Cabinet Member for Adult Social Care, Health and Housing presented a report on the Barking and Dagenham Housing Strategy 2026-2031.

 

The Strategy had been developed following detailed analysis and co-produced engagement with residents and council officers. The Strategy built on the Interim Housing Strategy 2025-2026. It provided a longer-term response to ongoing challenges such as housing supply and affordability, overcrowding, housing conditions and the safety of the borough’s housing stock.

 

The Cabinet Member advised that the Strategy, which was considered and endorsed by the Cabinet at its meeting on 16 February 2026, was structured around six strategic objectives:

 

·  Increasing the supply of genuinely affordable and accessible homes;

·  Improving housing affordability for local people;

·  Preventing homelessness and rough sleeping;

·  Supporting vulnerable residents to live independently;

·  Improving the quality and safety of all housing; and

·  Supporting prevention and wellbeing through partnership.

 

The Action Plan set out the specific commitments, delivery responsibilities and timescales under each of the objectives.  Key actions included delivering new affordable homes through Council-led development and strategic partnerships; reducing overcrowding and under-occupation; bringing empty homes back into use; investing in planned repairs, safety improvements, and energy efficiency works; and strengthening resident involvement in housing decisions.

 

Delivery of the strategy would be overseen through a Councillor-led Delivery Board, supported by a cross-service Officer Group to coordinate implementation across the Council. Resident insight would be embedded through a Scrutiny Group, ensuring lived experience informed delivery and ongoing improvement. These governance arrangements would support transparent and accountable implementation.

 

In response to a question, the Cabinet Member advised that a key action of the strategy was to review the allocations policy, which incorporated the housing register, and stressed the point that it was a housing register and not a waiting list.

 

Members commended the strategy, noting that the borough had a low number of households in temporary accommodation in comparison with other boroughs.

 

The Assembly resolved to:

 

(i)  Note the Housing Strategy Resident Engagement Summary, attached at Appendix 3 to the report, outlining the extensive consultation and feedback from residents and stakeholders that have informed the development of the Housing Strategy and Action Plan;

 

(ii)  Approve the Barking and Dagenham Housing Strategy 2026-2031, attached at Appendix 1 to the report, as the Council’s overarching housing plan for the next five years; and

 

(iii)  Approve the Housing Strategy 2026-2031 Action Plan, attached at Appendix 2 to the report, setting out the specific actions, targets and timelines for delivering the Strategy’s objectives.

Minutes:

The Cabinet Member for Children’s Social Care and Disabilities presented the Corporate Parenting Annual Report 2024/25, which provided a comprehensive summary of the activities that had taken place within 2024/25 to meet the Council’s Corporate Parenting priorities and promises to children in care and care experienced adults up to the age of 25.

 

Corporate Parenting responsibilities were a statutory requirement for Children’s Social Care and the Council as a whole, and performance in that area formed part of the Ofsted Inspection of local Authority Children’s Services (ILACS) framework.

 

The annual report provided a summary of activity over 2024-25 to meet the Council’s corporate parenting duties and promises to children in care and young people leaving care. The Barking and Dagenham Corporate Parenting Strategy 2023-25 set out five promises in relation to this:

 

1)  To make sure you get the best care;

2)  To look after yourself and keep you safe;

3)  To keep you healthy;

4)  To help you get the best possible education; and

5)  To help you be successful in life.

 

The Cabinet Member advised that to fulfil those promises, over 2024-25 a range of activities were carried out, including:

 

Ø  Putting participation at the heart of our approach, working closely with children in care and care leavers to improve what we do;

Ø  Launching our Lifelong Links service that brings together important, positive people in a young person’s life who they want to reconnect with, to provide them with ongoing support;

Ø  Opening another Mockingbird Hub, to create an ‘extended family’ for foster carers and children – a place to connect, share resources and access respite and training;

Ø  Providing new, specialist support on education, employment and training – creating new opportunities and positive outcomes;

Ø  Starting the lengthy process of developing our own children’s homes, so that we have more local support options that meet the needs of children in our borough; and

Ø  Supporting children to achieve education results at key stage 2 and key stage 4 that are above the national average for children in care.

 

In response to questions, the Cabinet Member advised that:

 

·  The complexity of needs for some children meant that specialist private care was required and therefore private placements were required.  There was a high level of children in foster care or kinship care within the borough; however, that was not always possible; and

·  The Care Leaver housing placement arrangements for those over the age of 18 was very strong, with a close relationship with the Housing Service.  A lot of young people were in semi-dependent housing, enabling to access support easily when required.  The Council had developed its own properties, alongside the charity Homes for Humanity, for those that needed ongoing care after the age of 18.  Where appropriate, care leavers were placed in affordable housing within the borough.

 

The Assembly resolved to note the report.

The Chief Executive presented a report on a review of the Council’s governance arrangements.

 

The report set out a number of proposed amendments to existing arrangements which would bring the Council’s committee structure more into line with common practice across other local authorities, such as the amalgamation of the Assembly and Ceremonial Council into “Full Council”, non-JNC level staffing appeals being dealt with at senior management level and revisions to the threshold for the Call-In process for Cabinet Key Decisions. Other proposals included changes to Cabinet support / advisory arrangements and the strengthening of several existing provisions within the Constitution to enhance clarity.

 

All of the proposed amendments to the Constitution and Council governance arrangements would take effect for the new Council in May 2026.

 

In response to a question, the Chief Executive confirmed that the proposals represented good governance which aligned with other authorities across the country.  The Chief Executive also commented that extending the membership of the Overview and Scrutiny Committee to 13 members (from 10) provided greater capacity for scrutiny.

 

The Leader commented that the savings achieved via the changes to the governance arrangements would provide better value for money, as well as strengthened, modernised and streamlined governance, creating greater opportunities for backbench members.

White Ribbon and Ending Violence against Women and Girls

 

Moved by Councillor Spoor and Seconded by Councillor Masson

 

1.  Note that White Ribbon Day is observed on 25th November and coincides with the United Nation’s Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women. We acknowledge the significance of this day in raising awareness about men’s violence against women and girls, and the importance of prioritising primary prevention to stop violence from happening in the first place.

 

2.  Recognise the ongoing need to address and prevent violence against women and girls, and the need to engage men and boys throughout society. This includes in education, in workplaces, communities and online.

 

3.  Acknowledge the critical role that we, as decision makers, play in shaping and implementing policies that prioritise preventing violence against women and girls and helping to create a more equal society where all individuals, regardless of gender, can live freely without the fear of violence.

 

4.  Call upon all Members to: 

·  Include the primary prevention of violence against women and girls within policy and strategies across council departments, alongside responses to gender-based violence. 

·  Develop and implement a comprehensive strategy for the Council to become White Ribbon accredited.

·  Support the implementation of Chapter 3 of the Istanbul Convention, which focuses on prevention measures, especially focusing on engaging men and boys through education, workplaces and leisure and sport organisations. 

 

5.  Encourage the effective implementation of international frameworks and conventions aimed at preventing violence against women and girls, like the Istanbul Convention and the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women. By endorsing this motion, we reinforce our dedication to preventing men’s violence against women and girls from happening in the first place.

 

Members of the Assembly spoke in support of the motion.

 

The motion was carried.
