Agenda item

Budget and Council Tax Setting 2026/27

  • Meeting of Cabinet, Thursday, 19th February, 2026 2.00 pm (Item 65.)
  • View the background to item 65.

Decision:

Cabinet:

 

(1)            recommended to Full Council that the Waltham Forest basic amount of council tax, including the levying bodies, will be increased by 2.99% in 2026/27;

 

(2)            recommended to Full Council that the 2% permitted increase for Adult Social Care will be applied bringing the total increase to 4.99% and resulting in a council tax of £1,876.45 per Band D property in 2026/27;

 

(3)            noted that the element of council tax charged by the Greater London Authority will be £510.51 per Band D property in 2026/27 which reflects an increase of £20.13 (4.10%);

 

(4)            recommended to Full Council that the overall council tax to be set for 2026/27 will be £2,386.96 per Band D property, which represents an overall increase of 4.80%;

 

(5)            recommended to Full Council that the projected budget (as set out in Appendix 1 to the report) for 2026/27 of £607.255 million is approved;

 

(6)            considered and noted the whole of the Strategic Director of Resources report made under Section 25 of the Local Government Act 2003 as set out in Appendix 2 to the report and, in particular, their determination in the Financial Implications of this report on “the robustness of the estimates made for the purposes of the calculations, and the adequacy of the proposed financial reserves”;

 

(7)            noted the amount of 82,991 (an increase of 208 over the previous year), as the Council Tax Base for the Council for 2026/27, in accordance with Regulation 3 of the Local Authorities (Calculation of Council Tax Base) Regulations 1992 made under the Local Government Finance Act 1992 (as amended);

 

(8)            recommended to Full Council that the following amounts to be approved for 2026/27 in accordance with Sections 31 to 36 of the Local Government Act 1992, as amended (the Act);

 

a)    Gross Expenditure

£955.480 million – being the aggregate of the amounts which the council estimates for the items in Sections 31A (3) of the Act;

 

c)    Net Expenditure

d)    LBWF Basic Amount of Council Tax including the Adult Social Care Precept

 

e)    £1,876.45 – being the amount at (c) above, divided by 82,991 (the Council Tax Base), calculated by the Council in accordance with Section 31B(1) of the Act, as the basic amount of its council tax for the year and includes the Adult Social Care Precept of £315.79.

(11)        noted that the relevant basic amount of council tax for a Band D property (excluding precepts but now including levies) for the financial year 2026/27 be £1,876.45, which reflects a 2.99% increase, plus 2% Adult Social Care precept permitted increase for 2026/27 and therefore does not require a referendum;

 

(12)        agreed that the Strategic Director of Resources and the officers nominated by him be authorised to finalise the necessary publicity as required by Section 38 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 and to promote the first day of the month instalment dates, and the six date options within each month for Direct Debit payers, as previously agreed;

(15)        agreed in accordance with the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 (Clause 76) to maintain a premium of 100% on dwellings that have been empty for one year from 1 April 2026;

 

(16)        agreed in accordance with the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 (Clause 77) to maintain a premium of 100% on dwellings which are “substantially furnished” but have no “resident” (e.g., second homes) from 1 April 2026;

 

(17)        agreed for the purposes of calculating entitlement to Housing Benefit, any war disablement pension, war widow’s or war widower’s pension which would otherwise be treated as income for the purposes of calculating entitlement to housing benefit should be disregarded in its entirety.

 

(18)        agreed the Capital Programme for 2025/26 to 2035/36 as set out in Appendix 10 to the report;

 

(19)        agreed the reserves strategy at Appendix 7 to the report and noted that the Strategic Director of Resources has delegated responsibility for the use of reserves throughout the year;

 

(20)        noted an application for Exceptional Financial Support (EFS) of £19 million from central government and the decision to utilise this support is the responsibility of the Chief Finance Officer;

(21)        agreed the flexible use of capital receipts policy, as set out in Appendix 3 and paragraph 3.51 of the report to invest in savings initiatives.  This will enable the funding of revenue projects and redundancy costs totalling £22 million from capital receipts, over 2026/27 and 2027/28. These costs will enable delivery of savings.

 

(22)        reconfirmed that on winding up of the Mears JV (More Homes Waltham Forest LLP) once any outstanding debt financing relating to the assets has been cleared the Balance of the Capital Return Allocation be transferred or paid to the London Borough of Waltham Forest Pension Fund. This is required to be reconfirmed annually within the budget report by the fund actuaries noting the original decision was made in February 2019 Council meeting.

 

Options and Alternatives Considered

 

The Council has a legal duty to set a balanced budget and to develop a financial strategy for the medium term.  The revised MTFS sets out potential challenges facing the Council in future years.

 

Minutes:

Councillor Douglas introduced the report and, in doing so, said that there had been an 80% increase in household temporary accommodation, a 40% increase in special educational need packages, and an 8% increase in adult social care costs had made it impossible for the Council to set a balanced budget for 2026-27 without some assistance from the Government in the form of Exceptional Financial Support (EFS).  He said that the Council were seeking to use that extra support to help ensure that residents’ council tax rises were below 5%.  He provided assurances that the Council had a very clear purpose for the use of that funding and a plan to pay back the loan as soon as possible. Councillor Douglas added that the additional funds would help protect the services that mattered most to residents, while balancing the budget in the short term.  At the same time, it would free up the time and resources necessary to accelerate the delivery of a major programme of change, which was based on a fundamental rethink of the way the Council designed and delivered its services for residents.  Councillor Douglas concluded by saying the Council were making a commitment to be in a fully secure financial position without the need for further borrowing and with a minimum of £30 million in reserves within the next three years.

 

The Leader stated that the Council had endeavoured to be transparent with residents about the situation that they were facing and had been clear about many of the pressures that were outside of the Council’s control, from borrowing rates to inflation to shortages, which had driven costs higher in some services.  She said that it was very clear that the Council intended to focus on the core services that they had.  The Leader also said that the pressures made it very clear that Council’s plan would balance the need to protect services for their most vulnerable, not only as they were a statutory responsibility, but also due to the fact that if the Council did not address prices and manage demand early, it could lead to higher expenditure for residents. The Leader provided assurances of the intention to balance the aforementioned with the need to transform every area of the Council. She went on to say that the budget before Cabinet was a balanced plan and allowed residents to hold the Council to account.  The Leader stated that it was recognised that the Government had worked quite hard in the previous eighteen months to understand the situation that local authorities were in however, one of the problems was how they could increase the share of money that went to local government, however, the amount of money required meant that many local authorities remained chronically underfunded. She said that it was important to continue to push the Government to reform services to enable local authorities to meet their responsibilities. She said that it was really good to see that the Government was looking at Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND, specifically, the deficits that local authorities had built up in relation to SEND and had a plan for dealing with most of that deficit.  The Leader drew attention to the ambitious capital programme which had delivered incredibly for their borough and pointed to the impact it was having, from schools to the delivery of services to town centres. The Leader then highlighted the importance of working collectively – Cabinet, senior leaders and each member of staff in the organisation, were crucial to the plan’s success and the future for the borough. She thanked everyone in the room for their hard work.

 

Councillor Loakes said that in terms of two particular areas that were identified within his portfolio: concessionary fares were being protected and funded that year; and a fair parking regime in the borough meant that the Council were able to provide, to over 30,000 residents across the borough, a free public transport offer that helped break isolation and kept them active, not just in the borough, but across London. He also explained that the North London Waste Authority levy was slightly less than what was originally budgeted for as residents had responded so well to changes in waste collection services, moving from weekly to fortnightly bin collection and introducing food waste. Councillor Loakes stated that residents seen their residual waste changes reduce (which was an expensive waste stream of which to dispose). He added that they had seen food waste tonnage increase dramatically, with over 8,000 tonnes already collected and recycled (which was a cheaper waste stream of which to dispose).  Councillor Loakes went on to say that there was an increase in mixed dry recycling waste and figures were lower than originally budgeted for and, as a result, the Council were exceeding expectations in the first year.  He concluded that such achievements were as a result of work the Council had undertaken with residents to do better for their climate and environment.  Councillor Loakes gave credit to all those in the organisation who had worked hard to get the Council into its current position. 

 

Councillor Khan referred to the Capital Investment Strategy, that would be before Cabinet next month for approval.  He spoke about the work undertaken over the previous ten years across the borough, working with the public and private sectors to drive improvement for residents. Councillor Khan said that despite the challenging financial climate, the Council would bring forward a capital strategy for the following ten years, which would present an ambitious programme cutting across all areas of the Council and very much focused on outcomes for residents. He added that a big part of their capital programme for the previous ten years had been housing-led growth in the borough.  Councillor Khan thanked Councillor Douglas for his leadership and his team for their hard work. 

 

Councillor Mithcell pointed out that over 60% of the Council’s total budget was spent on services, which include those for adults living with disabilities or long-term illnesses and said that the number of adults needing those services was increasing year on year.  She referred to the importance of the Council looking into how resources are targeted and how to ensure that elderly residents were kept safe and independent.  Councillor Mitchell highlighted the fact that the Council was facing rising costs and charges and she pointed out that that was the case for providers of services commissioned by the Council.  She then referred to the importance of the Council being given the opportunity to contribute to the Casey Review which aims to reform Adult Social Care in England.

 

The Leader added thanks to the Adult Social Care team for their hard work.  She also thanked Councillor Douglas for his leadership and Rob Manning and the wider finance team for their hard work.
