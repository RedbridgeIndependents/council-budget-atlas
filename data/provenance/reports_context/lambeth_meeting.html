Minutes:

Deputation 1 – St Judes School Street

Louise Thomas and Marilyn McKenzie gave a deputation on behalf of parents and families from St Jude’s Primary School regarding the delay to the implementation of a School Street outside the school. It was stated by the deputees that:

  • Lambeth Council had committed in 2023 to introduce School Streets at all feasible primary schools, with St Jude’s expected to be implemented in Autumn 2025, however the programme had since been paused due to capital budget constraints.
  • School Streets had been shown to reduce nitrogen dioxide levels during drop-off and pick-up times and supported safer journeys for children travelling to and from school.
  • Parents expressed concern that delays meant children continued to experience road danger and elevated air pollution levels outside the school during peak times.
  • Air pollution monitoring data from outside the school had indicated levels above the World Health Organisation’s recommended annual average during the monitoring period.
  • The deputees highlighted the health impacts of air pollution on children, particularly those with respiratory conditions such as asthma, and the wider benefits of encouraging active travel to school.
  • The deputation requested that the Council commit to implementing the St Jude’s School Street by July 2026.

Councillor Rezina Chowdhury, Deputy Leader (Sustainable Lambeth and Clean Air), in response stated that:

  • Reducing traffic around schools remained a priority for the Council, as this improved safety, supported healthier journeys and improved local air quality.
  • The Council had already delivered 45 School Streets serving 50 schools across the borough and remained committed to expanding the programme.
  • The intention was that all eligible primary schools not located on main roads would have a School Street in place by March 2027.
  • Following a review of the delivery programme, the Council intended to bring forward the implementation timetable for St Jude’s, with the aim of launching the School Street at the start of the new academic year in September.

Minutes:

Councillors Donna Harris, Scott Ainslie, and James Bryan spoke to the Constitution 2026/27 and raised the following points:

 

  • Councillor Harris expressed concern regarding the operation of the Council’s constitutional procedures, including the view that existing rules did not provide sufficient mechanisms to challenge statements made about previous budget decisions.
  • It was suggested that scrutiny and challenge within the Council Chamber were sometimes treated negatively rather than as part of the democratic process.
  • It was stated that the Council Chamber should strive for higher standards of debate and accountability.
  • Councillor Ainslie highlighted that the constitutional review followed a Local Government Association (LGA) Peer Review which identified significant issues within the Council, including concerns relating to financial control, governance and the effectiveness of scrutiny, and which also found that parts of the existing constitution were unlawful.
  • Concern was expressed that the review process had been rushed, with insufficient time for Councillors to fully consider the implications of the proposed constitutional changes.
  • Further concern was raised that some of the proposed changes, including those relating to Council procedures and the handling of motions, petitions and questions, could reduce opportunities for scrutiny and democratic accountability.
  • Councillor Bryan stated that the constitutional review had been undertaken through a cross-party working group involving representatives from all political groups, with the aim of strengthening governance arrangements.
  • It was noted that the review process involved discussion, scrutiny and consideration of external legal advice, with officers from Legal and Democratic Services contributing expertise throughout the process.
  • Criticism was raised regarding the conduct of the Green Group during the review, with the view expressed that attempts had been made to seek additional representation and resources through proposed changes to arrangements for determining the official opposition.

 

RESOLVED: unanimously

1.        To approve the table of proposed changes to the Council's Constitution (Appendix 1)

2.        To approve the updated Council Procedure Rules (Appendix 2 & 3)

3.        To approve the final draft of track changes of the Lambeth Constitution (Appendix 4) for publication.

Minutes:

Councillors Matthew Bryant, Paul Valentine, and Judith Cavanagh spoke to the Pay Policy Statement 2026/27 and raised the following points:

  • Councillor Bryant noted that the Pay Policy Statement highlighted positive developments including a reduction in the ratio between the highest and lowest paid employees and a reduction in the gender pay gap.
  • The importance of fair remuneration in maintaining a stable and motivated workforce emphasised.
  • Concern was expressed regarding the increase in the number of employees earning above £50,000 and £100,000, although it was acknowledged that the report was largely factual in nature and therefore there was no objection to supporting it.
  • Councillor Valentine also noted the progress in reducing the pay ratio between the highest and lowest paid staff and the narrowing gender pay gap, although further improvement was encouraged.
  • The important contribution of frontline staff, including librarians, care workers, social workers and youth workers, was acknowledged, with concern expressed that these staff were facing increased workloads and pressures in the context of service budget reductions.
  • Concern was raised regarding transparency within the Pay Policy Statement, including the absence of previously published information on staff earning over £100,000 and compliance with transparency requirements for senior salaries, with a call for clearer reporting to support scrutiny.
  • Councillor Cavanagh highlighted that the Pay Policy Statement, previously considered by Corporate Committee, was presented for approval and provided an opportunity to formally thank Council staff for their work supporting residents, organisations and businesses during a period of increased demand for services.
  • It was highlighted that Lambeth remained committed to fair pay as an accredited London Living Wage employer, with progress made in improving pay equity, including reducing the pay ratio between the highest and lowest paid staff from over 10:1 to around 7.6:1.
  • Changes to the Council’s workforce and pay bill were attributed to nationally agreed pay awards, a reduction in agency staff through conversion to permanent roles, the bringing of services back in-house, and a reduction in the senior management structure, alongside efforts to improve workforce diversity and reduce the gender pay gap.

 

RESOLVED: by 47 votes for, 0 votes against and four abstentions

Minutes:

Councillors Ben Curtis, Martin Abrams and Dr. Mahamed Hashi spoke to the Lambeth Statement of Principles for Gambling 2026-2029 and raised the following points:

  • Councillor Ben Curtis expressed that removing the “No Casino” resolution was a mistake, despite current national legislation preventing new casino licences in London.
  • It was noted that national legislation could change in the future and that retaining the resolution would provide a local safeguard should the legal position change.
  • It was argued that the resolution imposed no current cost or impact on existing businesses and that removing it could require a new policy process to reinstate similar protections in the future.
  • Councillor Abrams also raised concerns regarding the proposed removal of the “No Casino” clause from the Statement of Gambling Principles, noting that the clause had previously acted as a safeguard preventing the Council from considering applications for casino premises licences.
  • It was stated that the clause had received significant public support during the consultation process, and concern was expressed that it had been removed from the draft policy without a clear explanation or transparent justification.
  • It was argued that removing the clause could weaken the Council’s stance on addressing gambling harm and protecting vulnerable residents, particularly in a borough with existing levels of deprivation and gambling-related risks.
  • Councillor Hashi stated that the Council had written to the Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government seeking reforms to gambling legislation, including greater powers for councils to rely on local evidence, set opening hours based on local risk, and close betting shops that breach licensing conditions.
  • It was explained that the removal of the previous “No Casino” resolution reflected the fact that national legislation does not currently permit new casino licences in London and that the change aligned the Council’s policy with the existing legal framework.
  • It was stated that the revised approach aimed to strengthen protections for vulnerable residents and addressed gambling harms while supporting the Council’s wider commitment to safer and healthier communities.

RESOLVED: by 44 votes for, seven votes against and 0 abstentions
