Decision:

For the reasons set out in the report and its appendices Cabinet RESOLVED to Agree to note:

 

i)  The progress made since the LGA Peer Challenge in December 2023 and subsequent progress visit in October 2024, outlined in this report and its appendix.

ii)  That this report provides a progress update, separate to the usual quarterly Building a Fairer Newham performance report, which will be presented to Cabinet in July 2025 and will include progress updates as it relates to delivery against the Building a Fairer Newham Corporate Plan and implementation of the Transforming Newham for the Future plan.

iii)  That in response to the non-statutory Best Value Notice issued by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, the Mayor and Cabinet will be receiving quarterly Best Value progress reports to provide assurance on the Council’s progress against the areas highlighted as areas of concern. Additionally there will be regular reports presented to Full Council.

 

Alternatives Considered

 

a)  The alternative to not publishing current information would be a continuing lack of transparency about the Council’s performance. That would undermine the ability of members and residents to understand how well the organisation is performing, and whether resources are being used effectively.

 

Minutes:

The Mayor introduced a report that demonstrated the delivery of the Action Plan which included, as part of the Building a Fairer Newham, outcomes-based performance reporting cycle.

 

It was to be noted that the regular reporting process was to demonstrate the progress report against the LGA corporate peer challenge recommendations and the subsequent action plan was produced as part of the quarterly reporting on the outcomes based performance framework; however, in this instance it was a standalone agenda item.

 

In October 2024, the LGA Peer Review team revisited Newham Council to determine the progress made and in line with the LGA Corporate Peer Challenge process. They made further suggestions on progress requirements, which included: increasing pace of delivery against the Action Plan in key areas in light of the financial challenges facing the Council, and providing clarity on the implementation timeline for a number of other actions.

 

The Mayor highlighted that the financial sustainability challenge facing the Council was a consequence of temporary accommodation and also pressures in adult and children social care. Financial sustainability was critical in the medium-term financial strategy and assets disposal had strengthened the fiscal position presently.

 

The report highlighted some of the improvements in areas such as Housing and then areas that needed improvement. In May the Council was issued with a with a non-statutory Best Value Notice by the Ministry of Communities, Housing and Local Government (MCHLG), setting out areas that required coordinated focus and action to provide the government with assurances that change would be implemented.

 

The Mayor reiterated that Newham was determined to respond to all the changes that were required to be undertaken and at the same time remain committed to transparency, accountability and delivering better outcomes as part of the delivering a fairer Newham for all.

 

Cabinet Members confirmed they were pleased to see that the progress and pace had increased. The Chief Executive confirmed that the Transformation Board had also played a vital role in offering a highly experienced team with a depth of knowledge in Local Government to which central government also had recognised the enhanced role as part of the assurance that government required for the Best Value Notice. 

 

The Cabinet Member for Finance and Resources confirmed that the Board, alongside providing their reassuring role were working to support the organisation with specific focus on areas surrounding future financial sustainability, continued housing improvement and consideration of those higher cost areas of the Council and what could be done to accelerate any change.

 

Other comments and queries were made around Member training and development, financial planning and development, and how that refresh performance monitoring enabled the Council to reach key milestones more quickly. 

 

It was confirmed that it was a longer program that ensured for financial sustainability in terms of what was trying to be achieved and that was just a highlight because most of the actions were completed with only a few left to be completed by the end of the summer.

Decision:

For the reasons set out in the report and its appendices Cabinet RESOLVED to Agree to note:

 

i)  The Council’s progress to date on the development and implementation of its response to the Regulator of Social Housing’s report; and

ii)  The further plans to drive improvements and bring the service into full compliance with the regulatory consumer standards set out in Appendix 2, and summarised in Appendix 1.

 

Alternatives Considered

 

a)  Do nothing: given the C4 rating, it is not possible for the Council to do nothing. Under the terms of the Social Housing Regulation Act 2023, the Council is required to comply with the Regulator’s judgement and reach compliance with the Consumer Standards. C4 and C3 ratings are non-compliant, so in order to achieve compliance, the Council would be required to meet a C2 or C1 grade.

 

Minutes:

The Cabinet Member for Housing Landlord and Tenant Experience Improvements introduced the report that provided an update for Cabinet on the Council’s progress in improvements to its social housing management and maintenance and in ensuring Council tenants live in safe, secure homes with access to high quality landlord services.

 

Access to safe, secure homes, and high quality landlord services for tenants and leaseholders living in Council managed housing was of paramount importance. Housing formed the bedrock of people’s lives and had an impact on a range of other life outcomes.

 

The Regulator of Social Housing inspected Newham’s Housing Services in May 2024 and gave its judgement in October 2024, issuing the Council with a C4 grading against its consumer standards. This was the lowest grading possible and indicated serious failings in compliance across several key areas outlined in the report.

 

Since the publication of this judgement, Newham had embarked on an ambitious improvement journey which was starting to see results in addressing the significant failings the Regulator identified.

 

Newham’s Housing Services Improvement Plan as appended to this report. set out the roadmap for how the Council would deliver safe, secure homes for its tenants and leaseholders, how it would make sure that residents accessing its Housing Services were treated with dignity and respect and how residents could take an active role in shaping services and holding the Council to account within this important area.

 

Work to address failings identified by the Regulator and from resident feedback had been accelerated over recent months and this included its response to the Regulator of Social Housing's findings.

 

The proposed Housing Services Improvement Plan was discussed with the Regulator of Social Housing on 31st March 2025 and again on 1st May. The public-facing summary and current Housing Services Improvement Plan are attached at Appendices 1 and 2 respectively. The plan remains subject to revision as we continue our engagement with the Regulator, which has been ongoing since the publication of their C4 judgement.

 

Significant progress had been seen in addressing the repairs backlog, improving levels of electrical safety checks and improving knowledge about the conditions of the housing stock which would help to improve home standards.

 

There was also work taking place to identify and support the most vulnerable tenants with important fire safety measures and initial results of this work showed it was supporting residents to access further support from cross Council services. New leadership appointments had been made and external assurance partnerships were in place with an increased focus on getting the best outcomes for our residents.

 

After questions from various Cabinet Members, it was explained that the key and first thing that had been done was to establish a Resident Challenge Board, a group of residents who were holding the Council to account on the delivery of the Improvement Plan. Community Opportunities PDF 309 KB

Decision:

For the reasons set out in the report and its appendices Cabinet RESOLVED to Agree:

 

i)  The establishment of a Framework for Community Opportunities with a term of four years with the option to extend for a further four years - at an estimated annual value of up to £28.2m (£225.6m over the eight years). For Newham the annual value is up to £4.7m (£37.6m over the eight years).

ii)  Delegate authority to the Corporate Director for Adults and Health in consultation with the Lead Member for Health and Adult Social Care to admit suppliers onto the Framework, following a competitive tendering procedure, during the life of the Framework.

iii)  Delegate authority to the Corporate Director for Adults and Health in consultation with the Lead Member for Health and Adult Social Care to approve commencement of mini-competitions under the Framework and award contracts (including direct award) once the Framework is established. And an annual Impact and Progress report to be presented to the Mayor and Cabinet at the relevant Strategic Board of the Council in line with its internal governance arrangements.

 

Alternatives Considered

 

a)  Do nothing and continue with spot purchase arrangements.

 

Minutes:

The Lead Cabinet Member for Health and Adult Social Care introduced a report that sought permission to establish an Open Framework for Community Opportunities.

 

The Council had a legal responsibility to assess the needs of any resident or carer living in the borough who appeared to have a need for care and support; and to then determine whether those needs were eligible for provision from the Council, as stipulated in The Care Act 2014.

 

The proposal to establish a Framework for Community Opportunities offered several benefits including:

 

·  Enhanced flexibility and choice - by enabling suppliers to join throughout the Framework period, increasing competition and providing a wider range of options for residents. This flexibility ensures the Council could continuously adapt to changing needs and preferences;

·  improved quality - by setting minimum quality standards for suppliers, the Framework ensured that only high-quality services were offered. This helped maintain a consistent level of care and support for residents;

·  cost efficiency - by enabling control over cost and future uplifts, helping to manage budget pressures effectively;

·  alignment with Council priorities and the Adults and Health vision.

 

In discussion the following points, questions and responses were as follows:

 

·  The report was aligned with Council priorities for the adults’ health vision which was a people cantered approach.

·  This was not just about targeted services; it was about how there was flexibility for people to come in and out of community opportunities and was in line with both that vision.

·  The important elements were putting in place the framework that would mean that the Council was in a better position to support older residents and

those with disabilities to access the activities they wanted at times they wanted in ordinary spaces close to their homes.

·  Where possible and it was hoped that it would mean that it would negate the need for a commission service; however, when commissioning services, it allowed a provision for high quality value for money services that provided residents with a sense of purpose and connection that was really important for both their physical and mental well-being.

·  There was a real need to maintain a consistent level of care and support for residents in a time of increasing budgetary and national issues and the proposed structure would enable control over costs, future uplifts and maintenance of budget pressures.

·  The report aligned itself with Council priorities for the vision in terms of an adult’s health vision which was based on a people centred approach that targeted services but had a flexibility for people to come in and out of community opportunities and was in line with that vision.

·  It would need to be cross referenced with the Corporate plan and underpinned by the Building a Fairer Newham principles.
