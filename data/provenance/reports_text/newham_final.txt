3. Background 
 
3.1. The Interim Section 151 Officer, as statutory officer, is required by statute to 
advise members as part of the annual budget -setting report on the robustness 
of estimates and adequacy of reserves, that the Council must have regard to 
when setting the budget and Council Tax. This assessment is detailed in 
Appendix A.  
 
3.2. In summary the final 2026/27 Budget proposals balances; significant ongoing 
demand led, demographic and inflationary pressures of £59.860m; with total 
savings of £55.747m; and additional income, mainly because of the 
government’s Fair Funding Review increa ses of £44.293m. A balanced outturn 

=== PDF PAGE 8 ===
 
 
position requires the delivery of all the savings set in this report to time, value 
and profile. 
 
3.3. The impact of the Fair Funding Review is significant. According to the PLGFS 
Newham will, by 2028/29 attract £123.979m more per year than the baseline of 
2025/26. The report sets out the main changes in funding allocation that have 
impacted Newham, namely updated population and income deprivation data 
being used in the formulae.  
 
3.4. Exceptional Financial Support from central government to balance the 
proposed 2026/27 Final Budget is not required in the form of Capitalisation 
Direction (using Capital receipts) or additional Council Tax increase  to achieve 
a balanced Revenue position. This means that the Council will not be selling 
assets to fund the ongoing costs of delivering services, as it now has a 
Sustainable Balanced Budget for the next 3 years , and a sustainable 
approach to a new 5-year Medium Term Financial Strategy approach.  
 
3.5. Whilst robust and achievable the budget is not without risk and so the Council 
has also addressed Financial Sustainability and Resilience in this Budget 
includ ing increases to General Reserves and Contingency at minimum of 10% 
and 2% of Net General Fund Revenue Budget respectively over the MTFS 
period. This is the Council’s financial safety net and last line of defence   
 
3.6. The budget addresses Financial Sustainability in the 2026/27 Budget and MTFS 
and will continue this Financially Sustainable position, balancing its Budget in a 
sustainable way for 3 years – 2026/27, 2027/28 and 2028/29.  This budget 
demonstrates the improvement made to establish Financial Sustainability and 
stability as required by MHCLG in its non -statutory Best Value Notice to the 
authority in May 2025 1. 
 
3.7. Council Tax income will rise by £11.018m under this proposed budget. This is 
made up of increases in the number of homes in the borough, known as the 
Council Tax base, the reduction in support in the Council Tax Reduction 
Scheme, and the proposed increase of 4.99% Council Tax made up of 2.99% 
for General Council Tax, and 2.00% for the Adult Social Care Precept

9.5. Legal Implications 
 
9.6. The Council is required under Part 1, Chapter III of the Local Government and 
Finance Act 1992 (the 1992 Act) to set a council tax for the forthcoming year 
and confirm its budget estimates. In accordance with Section 30(6) of the 1992 
Act, the decision must be made before 11 March of the preceding year (i.e. by 
midnight on 10 March ) but is not invalid merely because it is made on or after 
that date. 
 
9.7. Each Member has a positive duty to ensure that the Council complies with its 
legal obligations, to set a lawfully balanced budget. In doing so, the Council’s 
prospective expenditure must not be likely to exceed its resources available to 
meet that expenditure.  
 
9.8.  The Chief Finance Officer has a legal duty to report to the Authority on the 
robustness of the estimates made for the purposes of the calculations and the 
adequacy of the proposed financial reserves. Cabinet must consider the Chief 
Finance Officer’s advice in this regard, when determining whether the proposals 
should be recommended to Council for approva l. 
 
9.9. The approval of the budget and calculation of council tax is a decision reserved 
to (Full) Council under Section 67(2)(b) of the 1992 Act. Should Council seek to 
reject or vary the budget in any material respect (in a way which is contrary to 
the 43 Executive’s recommended budget), then the statutory process set out in 
Part 4.3 of the Constitution must be followed.  
 
9.10. Cabinet and Council, in recommending and then setting the Council Tax, must 
have due regard to the impact those decisions will have upon the Council’s 
statutory public sector equality duty as set out below.  
 
9.11. In considering the recommendations in this report, Councillors must have due 
regard to the Sector Equality Duty as part of their decision making.

9.13. Equalities Implications 
 
9.14. The Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) under Section 149 of the Equality Act 
2010 requires the Council, when exercising its functions, to have due regard to: 
• the need to eliminate discrimination, harassment, victimisation and any 
other conduct that is prohibited by or under the Equality Act 2010;  
• the need to advance equality of opportunity between persons who share 
protected characteristics and those who do not, and;  
• foster good relations between those who have protected characteristics 
and those who do not.  
 
9.15. ‘Protected characteristics’ are: age, gender, race, disability, sexual orientation, 
marriage and civil partnerships, religion or belief, pregnancy and maternity and 
gender reassignment. The Council also recognises socio -economic 
disadvantage, care experien ce and health and wellbeing status as additional 
characteristics.  
 
9.16. Any budgetary decisions which subsequently need to be taken should be made 
in accordance with the duty set out above.  
 
9.17. Due regard means giving proper and focussed consideration of the impact of 
the decision on meeting its equalities duties. The primary focus should be on 
any adverse equalities implications that will arise from a decision and how they 
can be avoided and / or how they are mitigated in the decisions. The amount of 
focus on the duties will vary with each decision and how far they adversely 
impact on protected characteristics. 
 
9.18. Members should note that budget decisions on services form only an allocation 
of funds to a service or area; they do not constitute the final decision on service 
delivery. Where changes to service delivery are proposed by budgetary 
changes, a full equaliti es assessment will be undertaken before the final 
decision is made by Cabinet. It is to be noted that the Mayor and Cabinet have 
stated that for the purposes of the Budget setting process for 2025/26 and the 
MTFS period enhanced diligence will be applied t o saving proposals brought 
forward for consultation, engagement and approval.
