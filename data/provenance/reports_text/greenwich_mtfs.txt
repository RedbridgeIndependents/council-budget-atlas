Authority for the next four years (2026/27 to 2029/30). The MTFS balances

resources and provides the basis for decision making.

5. Autumn Budget 2025 
 
5.1 The Chancellor delivered the Government’s Autumn Budget on 26 
November 2025. The budget sets out the medium term path for public 
finances,. 
 
5.2 Whilst there weren’t a lot of announcements that directly impacted funding 
arrangements ahead of the provisional Local Government Finance Settlement, 
there were some announcements that indicated the direction of travel for key 
issues. 
 
5.3 The announcement that SEND funding will become the responsibility of the 
Government from 2028/29 is very welcome. This will alleviate the risk 
associated with the current statutory override position to one extent or 
another. We will have to await final details but indications are that there will 
be a requirement for local authorities to demonstrate that they have taken 
appropriate measures to minimise and/or reduce the existing deficit as a 
precursor to agreeing the final resolution and transfer of responsibility. 
 
5.4 Confirmation of Government backing for both, the extension of the 
Docklands Light Railway through to the Borough, alongside the potential 
establishment of a new town at Thamesmead were also good news. Again the 
details of how these infrastructure developments will be delivered and funded 
have yet to be disclosed but both will drive growth within Greenwich. 
 
5.5 The budget also announced the introduction of a High Value Council Tax 
Surcharge (HVCTS) to be introduced from April 2028, based on updated 
valuations to identify properties above the threshold, the surcharge will be in 
addition to existing Council Tax. New charges start at £2,500 per year, rising

8. Local Government Finance Settlement 2026/27 to 2028/29 and 
other updates  
 
Local Government Finance Settlement  
 
8.1 The 2026/27 Local Government Finance Settlement was approved by 
Parliament on 11th February 2026. It outlines funding allocations for local 
authorities for the next three financial years. The settlement marks the first 
time in this decade that the Government has illustrated multiple years’ worth 
of funding allocations, covering 2026/27 to 2028/29. 
 
8.2 The outcome of the Fair Funding Review was published which sets the 
underlying figures for the Fair Funding Assessment (i.e. the redistributed 
2025/26 funding totals). The long awaited change in funding distribution 
updates the relative needs assessment for local authorities, with the aim of 
ensuing authorities receive a fair level of funding to deliver services based on 
the needs of their residents. This is achieved via a redesign to data 
underpinning various relative needs formulae and is a mix of data updates and 
methodology changes. 
 
8.3 It was confirmed the council tax referendum limit will be maintained at 2.99% 
for local authorities, with social care authorities allowed an additional 2% 
social care precept. Six councils (five within London) will have no referendum 
principle applied in 2027/28 and 2028/29, allowing them to increase council 
tax above this level.

8.4 It was confirmed there would be a reset to baseline Business Rates collected 
by authorities, this is to be introduced from 1 April 2026 in conjunction with 
an updated national ratable value listing of businesses and new business rates 
multipliers which will affect the level at which businesses pay their rates. The 
funding effect of the revised business rates baseline for authorities is intended 
to be offset with new top-up and tariff amounts. The impact of these reforms 
does not change the outcome of how the Fair Funding Review redistributes 
2025/26 funding. 
 
8.5 Adult social care funding received by authorities in their 2025/26 core 
spending power has been redistributed using the Fair Funding Review, with 
the Local Authority Better Care Grant allocations top sliced out of social care 
authorities’ Fair Funding Assessment (FFA) to ensure 2025/26 funding level is 
maintained for this grant in 2026/27. The Department of Health and Social 
Care has announced that it will publish ‘notional’ adult social care amounts 
early in 2026, which will set out an expectation of how much of council 
funding should be spent on adult social care. 
 
8.6 It was confirmed that authorities in receipt of the Recovery grant (for which 
Greenwich is one) would receive a level of funding guarantee of their Core 
Spending Power (CSP). Eligible authorities would be guaranteed to have their 
CSP uplifted by 5%, 6% and 7% respectively in each year of the settlement 
compared to their revised 2025/26 baseline (this approximates to 5% + 1% + 
1% for Greenwich and most cases). This funding guarantee has a £35 million 
cap over three years for each authority; Greenwich has been allocated 
£33.8m in total across this period which represents 97% of the cap.  
 
8.7  The government maintains local government allocations shown as Core 
Spending Power (CSP) which reflects local authority funding position from 
government grant and possible council tax funding.

Capital Programme 
 
9.18 The need to increase the revenue provision for capital investment financing is 
due to the expansion of the capital programme and the resulting rise in the 
Capital Financing Requirement (CFR). The CFR reflects the total amount the 
authority needs to borrow to fund capital expenditure that cannot be covered 
by receipts, grants or contributions. As the capital programme, which includes 
one-off investments linked to prior year MTFS proposals, grows, this 
increases the annual costs of servicing this debt, including interest payments 
and principal repayments. For several years, the council’s treasury 
management budget has substantially underspent through its policy of under 
borrowing – this was reported as being temporary, pending actual borrowing 
occurring and the underspend unwinding. External borrowing is now 
underway and the costs are starting to be realised. To ensure financial 
sustainability and compliance with financial regulations, the authority must 
allocate sufficient resources in its budget to cover these rising capital 
investment financing charges. £9.0m is provided to meet these costs annually 
from 2026/27 onwards and further sums as required in to the MTFS.

14.1 The UK Economic Position impacts the delivery of the services throughout

sector. The performance of the UK economy can affect the Government 
funding received and the costs incurred to deliver council services. The key 
national economic indicators are summarised below.      
  
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) Growth 
 
14.2  The UK's GDP growth is modest and slowing, with Q3 2025 showing a 0.1% 
quarter-on-quarter rise and 1.3% year-on-year, driven by services but 
hindered by weaker spending. Four-quarter UK GDP growth is projected to 
fall a little in the near term, below its current level of 1.4%, before improving. 
The pickup is driven by the lagged effect of recent interest rate cuts, the 
gradual loosening in monetary policy embodied in the market-implied path of 
interest rates, improvements in wider financial conditions, and higher global 
demand.

16. Statement under s25 of the Local Government Act 2003 
 
16.1 Under s25(1) of the Local Government Act 2003, the s151 / Chief Finance 
Officer is required to include in the budget report their view of: 
 
• the robustness of the estimates made for the purposes of the budget 
calculations 
• the adequacy of the financial reserves. 
16.2 In turn, under s25(2), the authority, in taking budget decisions, shall have 
regard to this statement. 
 
 Robustness of the Budget Estimates 
 
16.3 The MTFS is a continuous process and the journey ending with the 
production of this report was started almost immediately upon completion of 
the equivalent report in 2025. Estimates are made at various points along the 
journey and these are reviewed and tested as time moves on to ensure they 
are still valid. They are still estimates and / or evidence of a plan that exists at 
a point in time, but which may or may not come to fruition for a variety of 
factors and could be within or outside of the council’s influence or control. 
These estimates cannot give guaranteed assurances, but give Members 
reasonable assurances that the budget is set on the best available information, 
evidence and data. 
 
16.4 The process for developing the MTFS starts with the estimated gap set out in 
the latest report (usually the budget report) and updated from there for 
economic, demand, price and other relevant factors, in consultation with 
departments.  In this context the budget presented for approval includes 
estimates about inflation, contractual uplifts, demand growth, additional 
income, fees and charges, changes to Government funding streams and 
potential savings and efficiencies. 
 
16.5 Whilst the Director of Resources ensures that the overall process drives 
forward, it is a collaborative effort including, the Lead Member for Finance, 
Resources and Social Value, the Leader, the Chief Executive, members of 
Cabinet, the Greenwich Management Team, senior officers and heads of

 Reserves 
 
16.13 Reserves are one off in nature and once used, they are gone. Reserves are 
generally held for three reasons: 
 
• a working balance to help cushion the impact of uneven cash flows and 
avoid unnecessary temporary borrowing – this forms part of general 
reserves 
• a contingency to cushion the impact of unexpected events or 
emergencies – this also forms part of general reserves 
• a means of building up funds, often referred to as earmarked reserves, 
to meet known or predicted requirements. 
 
16.14 There is no statutory definition of a minimum level of reserves, and it is for 
this reason that the matter falls to the judgement of the Section 151 Officer. 
In coming to a judgement on this matter the Section 151 Officer has 
considered the following matters:  
 
• Risks inherent in the budget strategy.  
• Risk management policies and strategies  
• Past financial performance against budget  
• Current budget projections  
• The robustness of estimates contained within the budget.  
• The adequacy of financial controls and budget monitoring procedures
