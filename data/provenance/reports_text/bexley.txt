3. OVERVIEW 
1.4. The Council is under a legal duty to set a balanced and sustainable budget and to 
set the Council Tax levels for the next financial year by 11 March 2026 at the latest. 
The setting of the budget is a decision reserved for Full Council. The Council’s 
Budget and Policy Framework required that a draft budget is issued for consultation 
with the relevant Overview and Scrutiny Committees to allow for their comments to 
be considered before the final budget proposals are made to Full Council. and the 
budget was scrutinised during committee meetings held in February. 
1.5. This report sets out key items for consideration by Members in setting the 2026/27 
budget and Council Tax of which full details were included in the report to Public 
Cabinet on 23 February 2026. The Cabinet is now putting forward its final budget 
and Council Tax proposals to the Council and the figures are set out in Appendix A 
to this report. 
1.6. The purpose of this report is to set out for Council the basis of calculation of and to 
set the Council Tax for 2026/27. The Localism Act 2011 made significant changes 
to the Local Government Finance Act 1992 (“the Act”), and now requires the billing 
authority to calculate a Council Tax requirement for the year, not its budget 
requirement as previously. 
1.7. This report recommends a 2.99% increase in the Council’s element of the Council 
Tax and 2.00% in respect of the Adult Social Care Precept. As a consequence, the 
proposed Borough element of Council Tax will increase by 4.99% for 2026/27. In

addition, there is an increase in the Greater London Authority element (detailed 
below in section 6 (b)). 
1.8. As the Council develops its detailed proposals it must continue to keep under review 
those key financial assumptions which underpin the Council’s Medium Term 
Financial Strategy; particularly as the Council becomes ever more dependent on 
locally raised sources of income through Council Tax and Business Rates. The 
Council is carrying a significant level of financial risk due to the rising cost of living, 
increasing inflation, and increased levels of demand and complexity of demand. 
1.9. The Capital Management Strategy meets the requirement to follow the Prudential 
Code for Capital Finance in Local Authorities, 2017 edition. It also adheres to the 
latest codes, in particular regulations 2 and 24 of the Local Authorities (Capital 
Finance and Accounting) Regulations 2003. 
1.10. A Capital Treasury Management Strategy for 2026/27, is required to be approved 
by the Council before the start of the financial year in accordance with the CIPFA 
Code of Practice for Treasury Management as well as setting a number of 
Prudential and Treasury Management Indicators for treasury management.

1.20. The Council can, subject to legislative constraints, increase its Council Tax rate 
through two mechanisms; the Adult Social Care precept and general inflationary 
increases. Each 1.00% increase in the Council Tax rate generates around £1.529m 
per annum, which equates to approximately 34 pence per week for the average 
Band D property.   
1.21. From 2016/17, in light of national spending pressures in Adult Social Care services, 
the Government gave an additional flexibility to authorities to put an additional levy 
on Council Tax, recognised nationally as the Adult Social Care Precept. The Local 
Government ministerial Policy Statement and the draft Local Government Finance 
Settlement announced that in addition to the proposed 3.00% Council Tax 
referendum limit, there would be an ability to levy an additional 2.00% Adult Social 
Care Precept. The 2.00% Adult Social Care Precept would raise approximately 
£3.059m in 2026/27.  
1.22. A 2.99% increase in the Council Tax general inflationary element and a 2.00% 
increase in the Adult Social Care would result in a total increase of 4.99%. This 
value is beneath the referendum threshold and therefore the basic amount of 
Council Tax proposed is not excessive and as such, would not necessitate a local 
referendum as set out in the Localism Act 2011. 
1.23. Subject to the agreement of the increases to the Council Tax levels contained within 
this report, the 2026/27 Council Tax Requirement (including Adult Social Care 
Precept) for the Authority is £160.596m.

7. SUMMARY OF LEGAL IMPLICATIONS 
1.26. This report sets out the recommendations from Public Cabinet on 23 February 2026 
to Full Council for the adoption of a lawful budget and the setting of the Council Tax 
for 2026/27. Members will note the Legal Implications Section in the Public Cabinet 
report of 23 February 2026. 
1.27. In considering this report, Members must bear in mind their fiduciary duty to the 
Council taxpayers of the London Borough of Bexley.   
1.28. The setting of the Council Tax is a matter reserved for the Council following 
recommendation from the Executive. 
1.29. Section 30 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 (“the LGFA”), as amended, 
requires the Council to set an amount of Council Tax for each financial year and 
provides that it must be set before 11th March in the financial year preceding that 
for which it is set. The matters detailed in this report enable the Council to set the 
Council Tax in accordance with the provisions set out in the LGFA. 
1.30. Sections 73 to 79 of the Localism Act 2011 amended the LGFA to set out the 
calculations that billing and precept authorities need to make in determining the 
basic amount of Council Tax. Section 31A of the LGFA, requires a billing authority 
to calculate its Council Tax requirement each financial year. In doing so, a billing 
authority is required to calculate its expected outgoings and income for the year. 
Where the authority’s expected outgoings exceed its expected income, the 
difference is the authority’s Council Tax requirement for that financial year (section 
31A(4)). 
1.31. Section 31B of the LGFA requires a billing authority to calculate its basic amount of 
Council Tax for the year by dividing its Council Tax requirement by its Council Tax 
base. The calculations undertaken pursuant to the above statutory provisions and 
the Local Authorities (Calculation of Council Tax Base) (England) Regulations 2012 
must be presented to and be subject to formal resolution by the Council.

1.32. Section 72(1) and Schedule 5 of the Localism Act 2011 (which inserts Chapter 1VZA 
into the LGFA), imposes a duty on billing authorities, major precepting authorities 
and local precepting authorities to each determine whether their relevant basic 
amount of Council Tax is “excessive”. If the basic amount of Council Tax is deemed 
to be “excessive”, there is a duty to hold a referendum. The threshold at which a 
Council Tax increase would trigger a local referendum was set at 3%. 
1.33. In addition, the Government has given additional flexibility to authorities with adult 
social care responsibilities to address the pressures within adult social care. This 
report informs Members of the 2.00% Adult Social Care Precept for 2026/27 in 
respect of adult social care. 
1.34. The Council has a number of statutory duties. However, even where there is a 
statutory duty, the Council often has discretion about the level of service provision 
taking into account need. Where a service is not provided as a result of a statutory 
duty but provided pursuant to the exercise of a power, the Council is not bound to 
carry out those activities. However, decisions to reduce or cease such services must 
be taken in accordance with administrative law. In relation to fees and charges, the 
Council must consider the potential impact financial pressures on its stakeholders. 
However, this must be balanced against the Council’s statutory duty to set a lawful 
budget. 
1.35. Section 25(1)(a) and (b) of the Local Government Act 2003 requires the Chief 
Finance Officer (Section 151 Officer) to report on the robustness of the estimates 
made for the purpose of calculating the Council Tax and the adequacy of the 
reserves. When considering what level of general reserve to hold, applicable 
legislation includes sections set out in the Local Government Finance Act 1992. 
1.36. The Council has a statutory duty to have regard to the report of the Chief Finance 
Officer (the Director of Finance and Corporate Services and Section 151 officer) 
when making decisions about its budget calculations. 
1.37. Members will note recommendation (13) in relation to the Chief Finance Officer’s 
opinion on relation to the robustness of the budget. 
1.38. With regard to Treasury Management, Members will note Section 1 of the Local 
Government Act 2003, (“the Act”) which provides for a local authority to borrow for 
any purposes related to its functions under Section 1(a) or the prudent management 
of its affairs under Section 1(b). Section 3(1) of the Act requires a local authority to 
determine and keep under review how much money it can afford to borrow. 
1.39. Section 12 of the Act provides that a local authority may invest – 
(a)  for any purpose relevant to its functions under any enactment, or  
(b)  for the purposes of the prudent management of its financial affairs 
1.40. The Act further provides that a local authority shall have regard to any guidance 
issued by the Secretary of State. The Secretary of State has made the Local 
Authorities (Capital Finance and Accounting) (England) Regulations 2003, as 
amended. Regulation 2 requires the Council to have regard to the Code of Practice 
(the “Prudential Code for Capital Finance in Local Authorities”) published by CIPFA. 
1.41. In considering the proposals set out in this report, Members must have due regard 
to the Public Sector Equality Duties set out in section 149 of the Equality Act 2010. 
Section 149 (1) requires “due regard” to the need to:

• Disability;

• Removing or minimising disadvantages suffered by people due to their 
protected characteristics; 
• Taking steps to meet the needs of people with certain protected characteristics 
where these are different from the needs of other people; 
• Encouraging people with certain protected characteristics to participate in 
public life or in other activities where their participation is disproportionately low. 
1.45. In discharging its Public Sector Equality Duties, matters to consider include:

• whether the cumulative effect of proposals in in relation policies/services
