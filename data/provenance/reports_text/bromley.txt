What we’re doing
 
A new dedicated home 
for children is opening in 
the borough, with a 
further home being 
planned. We are looking 
to ensure more of the 
borough’s children can 
remain local, closer to 
their support networks, 
while reducing our 
reliance on expensive 
placements and 
specialist providers from 
the private sector. 
New technology is being 
used in Adult Social Care 
to improve services while 
minimising costs, with 
Magic Notes, an Assistive 
Technology, saving 
£1.8m alone. Magic 
Notes helps free up time 
for our social workers by 
generating reports from 
secure recordings, so 
residents can beneﬁt 
from more attentive 
care assessments.
The Bromley Homes for Bromley 
People programme is building 
quality affordable housing 
across the borough to let to 
residents on the housing register. 
Five sites are already 
completed, most recently of 35 
homes in Orpington, with further 
sites in progress. The council is 
also acquiring housing as close 
to the borough as possible, 
while supporting residents facing 
homelessness in remaining in 
their homes wherever possible.

Message from the Leader of Bromley Council, 
Councillor Colin Smith 
Dear Resident,
 I am certain from time to time, that many of you will have read a news article about a huge win on the National 
lottery and pondered what you would do personally with however many million pounds were up for grabs. 
Well, I’ve been haunted by two similar questions recently; what more could Bromley do with an extra 112 
million pounds per year and what on earth are other London Councils spending their extra funding on?
This is the amount Bromley would receive each and every year with just the average Government settlement for 
London under the new funding arrangements, which amounts to about 32% of our annual net budget.
For some this may sound like a familiar story, as since the turn of the century Bromley has been losing out in the 
distribution of central government funding for local councils and we have been incessantly calling on 
Government to put this injustice right for Bromley ever since.
However, instead of answering these calls, especially in the face of the relentless cost pressures we’re facing, 
with a further £33m in extra costs projected for this year, the Government’s latest announcement does quite the 
opposite. Bromley will now be £22.2m worse off each year by 2028/29, rising to £30.5m factoring in 
projected inﬂation, which leads to the astonishing disparity above.
As frustrating as it may be, the Government has made its decision and isn’t going to change it. We are on our 
own. Again.
I hope some of the good work highlighted here that we have managed with great difﬁculty to ﬁnd the money 
for, gives you a sense of what more we could do with an extra £112m, be that the 8 refurbished libraries we 
have reopened in the last few years, the two transformations of leisure centres we are delivering in Orpington 
and West Wickham, or the new state-of-the-art health and wellbeing centre now operating in the heart of 
Bromley town centre.
Our focus, drive and vision remains undimmed – to continue driving down costs and carefully manage our 
ﬁnances over the longer term, whilst keeping your council tax as low as we possibly can under the 
circumstances, balanced against the need to prioritise the delivery of the services that residents want and 
deserve, to the standard you expect.
It is no easy task, but we press on.
Kind regards
Councillor Colin Smith

Bromley lose out in Government 
funding announcement
Bromley’s Government grant is being reduced by £22.2m per 
year by 2028/29, representing a 22% reduction in core funding 
and 28% in real terms factoring in inﬂation. This is a signiﬁcant 
reduction compared to London and even compared to Outer 
London, as Bromley would receive an additional £74.7m in 
2026/27 with the average settlement per head for Outer London.

6|   TACKLING LONELINESS  | ww w.bromley.gov.uk
Success in tackling loneliness 
Bromley’s leading Loneliness Strategy brings together the work of the Council, voluntary groups and 
partners on a wide-range of initiatives to tackle loneliness, which now includes an online directory of over 
1,500 related activities and groups. This work is paying off with Bromley now in ﬁrst place in London 
amongst residents stating they have as much social contact as they want, according to the Social Care 
Outcomes Framework. Recognition for Bromley’s Loneliness Champion was also received in the New 
Year’s Honours List!
   Bromley is continuing to develop 
support for the armed forces 
community. Local businesses and 
organisations are encouraged to join 
this journey by signing up to the Armed 
Forces Covenant, this is a nationwide 
pledge to treat with fairness and respect 
those who serve or have served in the 
armed forces, along with the wider 
armed forces community. 
Employment service
gets to work
Bromley Works, the Council’s new employment and skills service, has launched an initial 
programme to support eligible residents into highh-quality employment, in support of Bromley 
businesses and the local economy. The programme directly links residents seeking employment and 
employers with local vacancies, while offering targeted training and advice for those involved. 
   Support for local businesses is also provided through initiatives like Start Up Bromley, which has 
supported over 900 local businesses and entrepreneurs through the borough’s libraries with free 
access to facilities, and tailored advice and support. Elsewhere, the borough’s businesses are 
coming together as part of Opportunity Bromley, the borough’s inward investment service led by 
local businesses to help the local economy to thrive. 
Saving through 
smart investments
The Council's nationally recognised pension fund is a prime example 
of the careful investment the Council pursues to maximise value in 
support of service delivery. Consistently high returns over many years 
mean that the outstanding performance is generating an annual saving 
of £10m for the Council by reducing the employer’s contribution. This 
saving will go to supporting Council services, while ensuring the 
pension fund remains fully funded to protect the contributions Council 
employees make over their careers.

Delivering 
for Bromley 
town centre
The long-term future of the Churchill Theatre 
has been secured thanks to the sale of the site 
to Galliard Group, with the theatre’s current 
operator Trafalgar Theatres now able to 
develop the theatre’s fantastic offering and 
bring forward investment plans. 
   Elsewhere in the town centre, a state-of-the-art Health and Wellbeing Centre has now opened as part of 
Bromley’s wider Civic Centre Building, bringing local health services together in a welcoming new 
environment. Bromley continues to support an enhanced offering for the town centre, with the recent 
Planning approval of plans to reconﬁgure the former Debenhams unit expected to attract a range of new 
leisure activities for residents. 
Transforming 
leisure centres      
The Walnuts Leisure Centre reopens this Spring following a 
complete transformation, with an expanded gym, 
competition-length swimming pool, new café, sauna and steam 
room. The multi-million-pound investment delivers essential repairs 
and a new heating system, ensuring the centre is ﬁt for the future. 
Works are also underway on a brand-new West Wickham 
Leisure Centre following demolition works completed last year.
Loving our libraries
Refurbishment works have been completed on eight 
of the borough’s libraries in recent years, ensuring 
they have the provisions needed to continue 
supporting residents for years to come. A range of 
exciting new features are also available across 
these libraries, from the Makerspace with a 3D 
printers in West Wickham, to the Cubbie to support 
neurodivergent residents in Mottingham, with each 
library brightened by a colourful mural from artist 
Alice Pattullo. Works are now progressing on 
creating a new home for Bromley Central Library in 
a prime high street location for late this year.

Parks, roads and recycling
Caring for 
our parks
There are over 160 parks and 
open green spaces across the 
borough, which the Council is 
protecting and enhancing, 
including with investment having 
been delivered from the 
Council’s £1m Platinum Jubilee 
Fund. Pratts Bottom Open Space, 
                   
Widmore Road Recreation 
Ground and Husseywell Open 
Space are amongst the latest 
green spaces to beneﬁt from new 
playground equipment, with 
park improvements having been 
delivered across Bromley, as we 
continue to cherish the borough's 
green spaces. 
Ramping up 
road repairs
Bromley is more than 
doubling its road resurfacing 
programme to over £5m in 
the coming year, as potholes 
created by the torrential rain 
this winter continue to be 
tackled. A trial of new ‘super 
asphalt’ is expected to speed 
up this process, which unlike 
traditional hot asphalt can be 
laid in any weather condition 
in 20 minutes on average.
Leading London
for recycling
Bromley has for many years
been leading London in 
recycling. Around half of 
Bromley’s waste is now being 
recycled and almost zero 
waste goes to landﬁll, with 
over 60,000 tonnes being 
recycled or composted a year.  
Residents are thanked for their 
ongoing efforts in recycling and 
reducing waste that supports this 
record, which also helps to 
minimise costs in delivering 
these services for residents.
