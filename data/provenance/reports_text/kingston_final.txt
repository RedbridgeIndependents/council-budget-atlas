 Introduction 
 1.  This  report  sets  out  the  Budget  for  2026/27  including  the  Capital  Programme, 
 Housing  Revenue  Account  (HRA),  Schools  Budget,  and  the  Medium  Term 
 Financial  Strategy  (MTFS)  to  2029/30.  These  combined  seek  to  support  the  vision 
 and ambitious outcomes for Kingston’s communities, residents and businesses. 
 2.  The  Council  Plan  2023-2027  sets  our  vision  and  priorities,  providing  the  framework 
 for  decisions  about  how  we  plan  services,  allocate  resources  and  measure 
 progress.  This  plan  was  developed  from  the  administration’s  manifesto  and  insight 
 from  our  residents,  businesses  and  communities.  The  Council  plan  has  four 
 themes, these are: 
 ○  Fairer:  Children  and  young  people  have  a  fairer  start  in  life  and  all 
 residents  are  supported  when  needed  throughout  their  lifetime.  Inequality 
 is  reduced  and  every  resident  has  increased  opportunities  for  a  happy  and 
 healthy life. 
 ○  Safer:  Our  borough  stays  safe,  well  maintained  and  resilient  so  individuals 
 and communities can thrive and prosper. 
 ○  Greener:  We  are  a  greener  and  more  sustainable  borough  and  on  track  to 
 meet our net zero carbon targets. 
 ○  Together:  We  have  strong  relationships  with  partners  and  work  together 
 with our communities to achieve our shared ambitions. 
 3.  And  we  will  continue  to  transform  our  Council  to  become  more  efficient  and  deliver 
 in a smarter way together with our partners under a theme of  Future Council  . 
 4.  In  order  to  achieve  this  vision,  the  Council  will  set  a  budget  and  put  in  place 
 actions  and  mitigations  to  achieve  a  MTFS  that  will  provide  a  foundation  for  the 
 Council  to  achieve  these  key  outcomes,  and  ensure  that  the  Council  is  ready  for 
 the  future,  is  managed  effectively,  efficiently  and  responsibly  in  the  best  interests 
 of  our  residents,  partners  and  businesses,  and  delivery  is  supported  by  high 
 quality support services.

 Fina  ncial Context 
 10.  This  section  sets  out  the  main  national  macro-economic  and  public  spending 
 position,  the  current  position  in  respect  of  local  government  finance  and  the  impact 
 of  the  economic  cost  of  living  crisis  on  the  Council.  In  short,  this  is  one  of  the  most 
 challenging  times  for  local  government  finance  there  has  ever  been.  Demand  for 
 statutory  services  nationally  such  as  social  care  (for  both  adults  and  children  and 
 young  people)  and  temporary  accommodation  continues  to  increase,  as  is  the  cost 
 of  providing  those  services  and  the  Council  is  experiencing  pressures  across  all  3 
 areas.  Meanwhile,  the  level  of  funding  for  local  government  is  not  keeping  pace 
 with  these  increases  in  cost,  meaning  that  the  level  of  savings  required  to  be  made 
 to set a balanced budget, continues to increase. 
 National Economic Context 
 11.  The  national  economic  outlook  remains  challenging.  After  peaking  at  11%  in 
 October  2022,  CPI  inflation  is  around  3.6%  in  October  2025.  Following  a  mid-year 
 high  of  3.8%,  the  highest  level  since  January  2024,  inflation  remains  higher  than 
 the  Bank  of  England's  target  of  2%.  Living  costs  remain  high  for  residents  and  as 
 a  consequence,  demand-led  services  continue  to  feel  the  impact  of  those  who 
 need  extra  support.  At  the  same  time,  inflation  has  driven  up  the  cost  of  delivering 
 these services which leads to additional pressures.

 inflation reducing at a slower rate than previously forecast in March 2025.

 essentials  such  as  food,  fuel  and  energy  bills  in  particular  remaining  much  higher

 Local Government and Kingston Context 
 16.  Local  government  finances  have  been  under  pressure  for  a  number  of  years, 
 however,  there  is  a  recognition  that  local  government  finances  have  been 
 underfunded  in  comparison  to  the  pressures  arising  from  social  care  and  the 
 housing  challenges,  which  have  been  regularly  reported  in  the  national  media  and 
 have been subject to intensive lobbying from public sector bodies. 
 17.  Living  costs  remain  high  for  residents  and  as  a  consequence,  demand-led 
 services  continue  to  feel  the  impact  of  those  who  need  extra  support.  At  the  same 
 time,  inflation  has  driven  up  the  cost  of  delivering  these  services  which  leads  to 
 additional pressures. 
 18.  Demand  for  service  provision  in  Adult  Social  Care,  Children’s  Social  Care  and 
 Temporary  Accommodation  continue  to  remain  acute.  Ongoing  market  pressures 
 coupled  with  demand  for  these  services  has  been  driven  by  demographic 
 changes,  rising  complexity  of  needs  as  well  as  the  long  term  effect  of  the 
 pandemic.  Kingston’s  population  grew  by  5%  between  2011  and  2021  and  trends 
 indicate  an  ageing  population.  These  services  account  for  a  significant  proportion 
 of  the  Council’s  budget  and  therefore  the  cost  and  demand  pressures  represent  a 
 significant financial risk. 
 19.  The  Council  has  seen  a  higher  than  anticipated  number  of  homelessness 
 approaches,  greater  than  forecast  losses  of  Private  Sector  Leasing  properties  and 
 longer  lead  in  times  to  deliver  some  of  the  anticipated  mitigating  actions.  This  has 
 resulted  in  a  greater  reliance  on  the  use  of  more  expensive  Nightly  Paid 
 Accommodation  (NPA).  Children’s  Social  Care  are  seeing  a  ten  fold  increase  in 
 complex  cases  compared  to  2018/19.  Adult  Social  Care  services  are  facing  high 
 unit  costs  especially  for  residential  care  for  the  over  65s,  combined  with  rising 
 demand  and  complexity  for  care  packages,  particularly  in  Mental  Health  and 
 Learning disabilities services is putting the service under increasing pressure.

 Settlement 2026/27 - 2028/29 
 21.  Following  several  years  of  single  year  settlements,  the  2025  Autumn  Budget 
 introduced  extensive  reforms  to  the  business  rates  system  and  local  government 
 funding.  These  changes  involve  a  fundamental  restructuring  of  how  business  rates 
 are  calculated  and  retained,  alongside  new  mechanisms  for  local  authority  funding 
 through the Fair Funding Review FFR).. 
 22.  The  settlement  implements  the  outcome  of  the  FFR,  which  redistributes  resources 
 based  on  updated  assessments  of  relative  needs  and  local  taxbase  strength.  The 
 financial  settlement  for  2026/27  was  the  first  time  this  decade  that  the  Government 
 has  illustrated  multiple  years  worth  of  funding  allocations,  although  these  are  not 
 confirmed until the future settlements. 
 23.  The  Fair  Funding  Assessment  (FFA)  distributes  the  outcome  of  the  FFR,  replacing 
 the  Settlement  Funding  Assessment.  An  authorities  share  of  the  total  to  be 
 distributed is based on 
 ●  The  starting  position  for  2025/26  to  determine  the  size  of  the  pot.  This  is 
 an  estimation  of  actual  business  rates  income  plus  grants  which  are  in 
 scope of the Fair Funding Review. 
 ●  The  calculated  ‘target’  amounts  for  2026/27  to  2028/29,  determined  by 
 splitting  the  total  pot  using  an  assessment  of  relative  needs  and  relative 
 strength of the local tax base which was the FFR. 
 ●  Transitional  arrangements  are  in  place  in  2026/27  and  2027/28  which  will 
 see  authorities  move  towards  their  target  shares  of  the  FFR  over  the  three 
 year period, a 1/3rd at a time, reaching full target share in 2028/29. 
 ●  At  this  time,  the  LA  Better  Care  Grant  (LA  BCG)  is  topsliced  so  the  FFA 
 and  LA  BCG  is  the  FFA  as  these  amounts  are  to  be  reviewed  for  2027/8 
 onwards following the review of the Better Care Fund policy. 
 ●  Funding  Floor  amounts  for  2026/27  to  2028/29  to  ensure  that  authorities 
 get a minimum funding increase. 
 24.  The  Fair  Funding  Assessment  is  split  across  two  funding  streams,  Business  Rates 
 Retention and the Revenue Support Grant (including the LA BCG). 
 25.  Since  its  introduction  in  2013/14,  the  Business  rates  Retention  Scheme  (BRRS) 
 has  been  stable,  with  the  only  change  due  to  Revaluation  (2017  and  2023).  For

 2026/27  the  scheme  will  see  wide  ranging  changes  relating  to  business  rates  as  a 
 tax and the resources that an authority can expect to receive. 
 26.  For  2026/27  the  business  rates  tax  base  has  been  revalued  and  a  full  reset  of  the 
 Business  Rates  Retention  System  will  be  implemented  for  the  first  time.  The 
 system  will  also  expand  to  five  multipliers  (from  2)  which  introduces  permanently 
 lower  rates  for  the  Retail,  Hospitality  and  Leisure  sector  which  will  be  funded  by 
 new  higher  rates  for  high-value  properties  which  have  a  rateable  value  of  £0.50m 
 or more. 
 27.  The  Fair  Funding  review  has  also  consolidated  a  number  of  specific  grants  within 
 an  authority's  core  spending  power.  These  are  detailed  in  paragraphs  116  to  125 
 of this report. 
 28.  The  Government  has  confirmed  that  it  will  maintain  the  core  referendum  principles 
 established  in  2025/26  throughout  the  mulit-year  settlement  period  (2026/27  to 
 2028/29)  and  Councils  can  increase  Council  tax  by  a  maximum  of  up  to  3% 
 without  the  need  to  hold  a  public  referendum.  The  threshold  at  which  Councils  can 
 levy  an  additional  Social  Care  Precept  (to  help  cover  the  cost  of  providing  care 
 services)  also  remains  at  2%.  Therefore  the  total  maximum  increase  without  a 
 referendum  is  currently  4.99%.  This  level  of  increase  is  assumed  in  establishing 
 the  council’s  core  spending  power  and  this  has  been  assumed  over  the  full  period 
 of the MTFS

 Reserves 
 33.  The  Council’s  Medium  Term  Financial  Strategy  (MTFS)  continues  to  ensure  that 
 financial  resilience  is  prioritised.  The  Council  is  committed  to  maintaining  sufficient 
 reserves  to  ensure  that  the  Council  has  relevant  resources  in  place  to  meet  future 
 policy  investment  commitments  and  to  provide  a  strong  financial  basis  to  protect

 Dedicated Schools Grant 
 37.  The  structural  overspend  and  shortfall  of  funding  within  the  Dedicated  Schools 
 Grant  (DSG)  for  children  with  special  educational  needs  or  disabilities  still  gives 
 rise  to  a  significant  financial  challenge.  The  November  budget  announcement 
 confirmed  that  the  government  would  extend  the  DSG  override  until  the  end  of 
 2027/28  and  that  once  this  statutory  override  concludes,  it  does  not  expect  local 
 authorities to fund future special educational needs costs from their general funds. 
 38.  Whilst  the  override  was  always  intended  to  be  a  temporary  measure,  the  national 
 cumulative  Dedicated  Schools  Grant  (DSG)  deficit  across  all  local  authorities  in 
 England  currently  stands  at  approximately  £3.15  billion.  The  deficit  is  projected  to 
 increase  further,  with  forecasts  suggesting  it  could  reach  £5  billion  by  the  end  of 
 the  2025/26  financial  year.  The  cumulative  deficit  on  Kingston’s  DSG  reserve  is 
 estimated to be approximately £21m by 31 March 2026. 
 39.  Following  engagement  with  the  DfE  over  a  number  of  years,  the  Local  Authority 
 signed  a  Safety  Valve  Funding  Agreement  in  March  2021.  This  agreement 
 provided  that  the  Council  could  claim  an  additional  £30.00m  in  revenue  funding 
 over  five  years  to  support  the  position.  The  successful  claim  of  the  additional  grant 
 was  conditional  on  the  successful  implementation  of  the  SEND  Futures  Plan.  By 
 December  2024  the  Council  had  successfully  received  the  full  £30.00m.  The 
 SEND  Futures  Plan  is  the  borough's  partnership  plan  to  both  improve  high  needs 
 education  services  and  move  annual  expenditure  more  in  line  with  the  annual 
 DSG  grant  allocation  and  aims  to  bring  education  spending  more  in  line  with  the

 Introduction and context 
 41.  Section  151  of  the  Local  Government  Act  1972  requires  local  authorities  to  make 
 arrangements  for  the  ‘proper  administration  of  their  financial  affairs’  and  to  appoint 
 a  Chief  Financial  Officer  (CFO)  to  have  responsibility  for  those  affairs.  In  Kingston, 
 the  statutory  S151  position  is  held  by  the  Executive  Director  of  Corporate  Services 
 and  this  section  of  the  budget  report  is  written  from  her  professional  viewpoint  as  a 
 statutory officer. 
 42.  The  CFO  must  exercise  a  professional  responsibility  to  intervene  in  spending 
 plans  in  order  to  maintain  the  balance  of  resources  so  that  the  authority  remains  in 
 sound financial health. 
 43.  Section  25  of  the  Local  Government  Act  2003  (LGA  2003)  imposes  a  duty  on  the 
 CFO to report formally to full Council on the following matters: 
 ○  The robustness of the estimates made for the purpose of calculations (to 
 set the Council Tax); and 
 ○  The adequacy of the proposed financial reserves. 
 44.  The  council  is  required  to  set  a  balanced  budget  each  year  and  a  minimum  level  of 
 balances  and  reserves  must  be  specified  within  the  budget,  to  be  taken  account  of 
 when setting the budget requirement. 
 45.  In  setting  the  budget  the  council  should  have  regard  to  the  strategic  and 
 operational  risks  it  is  facing.  The  S151  officer  (CFO)  has  undertaken  this  risk 
 assessment  and  has  taken  into  account  the  risks,  issues  and  mitigations  as 
 detailed within the rep  ort and Annex 1. 
 46.  Looking forward there are a number of risks on the horizon including: 
 ○  Pay, Price and Contract Inflation; 
 ○  Further volatility in interest rates; 
 ○  Continued increase in demand led spending pressures; 
 ○  Ongoing impact of Policy changes including SEND Reform and the impact 
 of the removal of the DSG Override; 
 ○  Any impact from the Business Rates Reset and update to Valuations list 
 and reliefs; 
 ○  Further changes in central government grant funding; and 
 ○  Non delivery of savings delivery plans and future Transformation plans to 
 address the ongoing significant budget gap across the period of the MTFS.

 Robustness of estimates 
 52.  Unlike  many  authorities  nationally,  Kingston  has  all  prior  years  of  accounts  signed 
 off  with  an  unqualified  opinion.  This  provides  a  verified  and  stable  baseline  for  the 
 2026/27  budget,  ensuring  that  our  starting  reserve  balances  and  "outturn"  figures 
 are not subject to the estimation errors currently prevalent in the sector. 
 53.  The  budget  and  medium  term  financial  strategy  are  forward  looking  pieces  that 
 therefore  rely  on  the  inclusion  of  estimates  of  what  may  happen  over  that  period. 
 The  strategic  leadership  team  have  worked  collaboratively  to  develop  estimates  of 
 demand  and  cost  based  on  the  data  available  and  have  provided  challenge  to 
 assumptions made about the future, particularly on demand-led growth. 
 54.  During  2025/26  the  Council  has  continued  to  see  demand  for  services  increase 
 significantly,  to  levels  beyond  those  that  could  have  been  reasonably  expected 
 when  the  budget  was  set.  Estimates  have  been  made  in  respect  of  the  future  level 
 of  demand,  particularly  for  adults  and  children’s  social  care  and  temporary 
 accommodation,  but  no  guarantee  can  be  given  that  demand  for  services  won’t 
 continue  to  rise  above  expectations.  Work  to  deliver  transformation  in  how  we 
 support  those  most  in  need  is  ongoing  and  will  be  vital  in  order  to  mitigate  further 
 increases in the future. 
 55.  Whilst  assumptions  have  been  made  that  the  level  of  inflation  will  remain 
 reasonably  static  over  the  medium  term,  the  budget  recognises  that  there  is  often 
 a  lag  in  timing  between  the  current  headline  inflation  rate  and  when  that  is  applied 
 to  contracts.  It  also  remains  the  case  that  where  growth  in  demand  for  services 
 outstrips  growth  in  supply  then  this  is  also  likely  to  lead  to  further  cost  increases. 
 Both  these  points  are  particularly  pertinent  to  expenditure  on  social  care  and 
 temporary  accommodation.  The  assessed  level  of  risk  around  inflation  is  included 
 in Annex 1.

 implementation,  this  has  been  identified  as  having  a  part  year  effect  in  the  first

 Adequacy of Reserves 
 59.  Reserves  are  held  to  help  ensure  the  Council  has  an  appropriate  level  of  financial 
 resilience  to  potential  future  financial  shocks  and  uncertainties  and  is  more  able  to 
 meet  the  costs  of  items  where  the  pattern  of  expenditure  is  uneven  or  timing  is 
 unknown.  The  general  fund  balance  addresses  the  wider  spectrum  of  unknown 
 risks  (as  the  HRA  balance  does  for  the  ringfenced  HRA  account)  whilst  earmarked 
 reserves are set aside for specific risks or future priorities.

 and  take  into  account  not  just  the  short  term  pressures  to  balance  the  budget,  but 
 the longer term risks to the Council's financial sustainability.

 Conclusion 
 69.  There  has  been  a  significant  and  ongoing  level  of  scrutiny  and  challenge  exercised 
 by  Officers  and  Portfolio  Holders  throughout  the  2026/27  budget  setting  process. 
 The  proposed  budget  for  2026/27  is  based  on  what  can  be  reasonably  known  and 
 assumed  at  the  time  of  setting  the  budget  and  with  an  acceptance  that  this 
 includes  uncertainty  and  risk  which  may  impact  adversely  during  the  2026/27 
 financial year. 
 70.  In  reaching  my  opinion  on  the  Council’s  financial  sustainability,  I  have  reviewed 
 our  performance  against  the  CIPFA  Financial  Resilience  Index  (latest  2025/26 
 data  release).  This  index  provides  a  range  of  indicators  that  highlight  the  Council’s 
 relative exposure to financial stress compared to our statistical peer group. 
 71.  On  this  basis  the  S151  officer  is  able  to  offer  assurance  on  the  robustness  of 
 estimates  in  accordance  with  Section  25  of  the  Local  Government  Act  2003.  This 
 is  predicated  by  the  need  to  maintain  as  a  minimum  the  level  of  reserves  currently 
 held by the council. 
 72.  Given  the  ongoing  growth  in  demand  and  cost  for  statutory  services;  the 
 uncertainty  regarding  accumulated  DSG  Deficit  and  requirements  under  SEND 
 Reform  and  other  potential  changes  in  regulatory  requirements,  the  S151  Officer 
 advises  in  line  with  Section  25  of  the  2003  Act  that  the  current  level  of  the  General 
 Fund  Balance  and  the  level  of  reserves  are  sufficient  for  2026/27  but  will  need  to 
 be kept under close review and scrutiny. 
 73.  However,  the  Council  is  not  immune  to  the  national  "policy  lag"  regarding  SEND 
 funding  and  the  HRA  rent  formula.  The  2026/27  budget  is  balanced,  but  its 
 long-term  viability  depends  on  the  successful  delivery  of  the  £3.43m  savings 
 program  and  a  favorable  national  resolution  to  the  DSG  deficit  before  the  2028 
 statutory  override  expires.  T  he  council  will  need  to  make  difficult  decisions  to  be 
 able  to  continue  to  make  further  significant  budget  reductions  through  efficiencies, 
 income  generation  and  transformational  activity  to  address  the  longer  term 
 financial  sustainability  of  the  council.  Further  proposals  will  need  to  be  brought 
 forward  in  a  timely  manner  for  decisions  in  2026/27  in  order  to  address  the  budget 
 gap for 2027/28 outside of the budget cycle. 
 74.  Any  amendments  to  the  budget  proposals  which  reduces  the  overall  level  of 
 reserves available may cause this advice to change.

 Climate action 
 74.  Kingston  Council  remains  committed  to  reducing  direct  greenhouse  gas  emissions 
 from  Council  activities  by  2030.  This  includes  emissions  from  energy,  water,  and 
 refrigerants  paid  for  by  the  Council  across  buildings  and  public  street  lighting,  all 
 vehicles  owned  or  leased  by  the  Council  and  all  staff  business  travel.  Actions  that 
 will  be  needed  to  reach  this  goal  will  not  only  benefit  and  protect  our  local  area  and 
 communities  but  also  will  ensure  that  the  Council  contributes  fairly  to  global  efforts 
 to address the current climate emergency. 
 75.  Improvements  in  the  energy  efficiency  of  Council  buildings,  street  lighting,  fleet 
 and  travel,  combined  with  the  increase  in  renewable  electricity  supply  from  the 
 national  grid  have  already  led  to  a  39%  reduction  in  comparable  emissions  in 
 2024/25 since the baseline year of 2017/18. 
 76.  Efforts  continue  with  further  installations  of  high  efficiency  LED  street  lighting  in 
 heritage  style  streetlights  across  the  Borough.  Electricity  use  from  street  lighting 
 has  already  halved  compared  to  2022/23,  has  reduced  carbon  emissions  by  450 
 tonnes  and  has  significantly  reduced  electricity  costs.  The  Council’s  fleet  is  now 
 over  90%  electric,  including  one  of  the  first  electric  waste  and  recycling  collection 
 fleets  in  the  country.  The  next  big  area  of  emissions  to  tackle  are  those  generated 
 from  the  Council’s  corporate  buildings  and  social  housing  blocks  that  make  up 
 76%  of  Council  operation  emissions.  Improving  energy  efficiency  and  looking  at 
 alternatives  to  energy  created  from  fossil  fuels  will  play  a  significant  part  of  the 
 journey  to  net  zero  as  well  as  help  to  ensure  that  public  buildings  remain  fit  for 
 purpose into the future. 
 77.  The  faster  that  reductions  can  be  made,  the  greater  the  contribution  to  mitigating 
 the  wider  impacts  of  climate  change.  National  efforts  to  move  electricity  to  be 
 “clean  power”  by  2030  will  mean  that  replacing  gas  use  in  council  owned  buildings 
 with  electrical  alternatives,  where  feasible,  will  be  vital  to  reduce  operational 
 carbon  emissions  to  net  zero.  The  Council  will  be  unable  to  meet  these  costs 
 directly  without  Central  Government  funding  in  support  and  the  Council  continues, 
 alongside  partners,  to  lobby  Government  to  provide  an  extension/alternative  to  the 
 currently paused Public Sector Decarbonisation Programme.

 79.  The  joint  aims  of  reducing  carbon  emissions  and  reducing  building  operational 
 costs  are  already  informing  major  projects  such  as  the  new  Kingston  Leisure

 technology  so  will  have  no  gas  use.  Plans  for  the  Guildhall  refurbishment  and 
 expansion will also include no new gas. 
 80.  By  including  carbon  and  wider  climate  related  questions  within  recent  large 
 contract  procurement  processes,  the  Council  is  accelerating  efforts  to  reduce 
 carbon  emissions  across  the  Borough.  Successful  contractors  are  now  partnered 
 with  the  Council  to  assist  in  reaching  operational  net  zero  while  also  increasingly 
 adopting  their  own  internal  targets  to  reduce  carbon  emissions  from  their 
 operations  in  the  Borough.  This  process  allows  further  leveraging  of  the  Council's 
 spending influence for environmental and societal improvement. 
 81.  Reducing  Council  carbon  emissions  will  not  only  reduce  operational  costs  but  will 
 also  avoid  future  carbon  offsetting  payments.  While  the  Council  aims  to  reach  as 
 practicably  close  to  Net  Zero  as  possible,  it  recognises  that  there  are  some 
 unavoidable  emissions  that  will  have  to  be  offset.  The  average  cost  for  offsetting  a 
 tonne  of  carbon  is  currently  around  £83.  Total  direct  carbon  emissions  from 
 Council  operations  in  2024/25  were  4,198  tonnes  so  therefore  fully  offsetting 
 current  emissions  would  cost  in  the  region  of  £0.35m  annually.  The  price  of 
 offsetting  a  unit  of  carbon  is  expected  to  rise  year  on  year  creating  an  additional 
 benefit to reducing emissions in avoiding these costs in the future.

 Council Tax 
 105.  The  Council  Tax  which  residents  in  the  borough  pay  comprises  two  elements,  one 
 is  the  tax  levied  by  the  Council  to  fund  its  services  and  the  other  is  an  amount 
 collected  on  behalf  of  the  Greater  London  Authority  (GLA)  and  paid  over  to  them. 
 The  final  decision  on  the  level  of  Council  Tax  levied  by  Kingston  will  be  taken  by 
 Members at the Budget Council meeting on 26 February 2026. 
 106.  The  level  of  income  collected  through  Council  Tax  is  determined  by  the  level  of 
 Council  Tax  charged  and  the  number  of  properties  upon  which  that  Council  Tax  is 
 chargeable  -  this  is  known  as  the  tax  base.  The  tax  base  is  determined  by 
 assessing  the  number  of  chargeable  dwellings  in  the  borough  and  their  Council 
 Tax  band,  allowing  for  discounts  or  exemptions  and  estimating  the  number  of  new 
 properties  that  are  likely  to  be  added  in  the  borough  during  the  year.  The  taxbase 
 directly affects the general fund income for the Council.

 Inflation 
 135.  A  key  element  in  the  budget  preparation  process  is  building  in  an  appropriate 
 allowance  for  inflation.  The  draft  budget  presented  continues  to  restrict  inflationary 
 increases  to  cost  elements  where  there  is  an  unavoidable  contractual  commitment 
 only.  Inflation  rates  have  been  higher  than  the  Government  target  of  2%  during 
 2025  but  the  forecast  for  CPI  in  2026  is  2.5%,  before  decreasing  to  the  target  of 
 2% in 2027, which is a year later than expected. 
 136.  Pay  inflation  has  been  allowed  for  within  the  budget,  based  on  estimated  rates 
 consistent  with  previous  years,  as  no  decision  has  yet  been  made  in  respect  of 
 pay  awards  to  local  government  staff  for  2026/27  and  is  not  expected  before  the 
 start of the financial year. 
 137.  An  increase  of  3%  has  been  allowed  for  in  the  budget  but  this  has  been  held 
 centrally  to  be  allocated  once  the  national  pay  negotiations  have  concluded. 
 Service  budgets  will  receive  the  necessary  increase  in  budget  for  2026/27  to 
 reflect the pay award once agreed. 
 138.  Non-pay  inflation  has  been  allowed  in  line  with  contractual  obligations,  however,  in 
 most  cases  these  costs  have  been  increased  by  3%  slightly  below  the  current 
 Consumer  Price  Index  (CPI)  inflation,  based  on  the  anticipation  of  further 
 reductions in the current CPI rate prior to the start of 2026/27..

 enclosure listing the full programme for the 4 year period 2026/27-2029/30. 
 162.  The  capital  programme  prioritises  Council  and  resident  needs,  featuring  significant 
 investment  in  housing.  This  includes  ongoing  Housing  Revenue  Account  (HRA) 
 funding  for  the  regeneration  of  the  Cambridge  Road  Estate  (CRE),  Acre  Road,  and 
 the  Small  Sites  2  programme.  Behind  housing,  further  investment  is  also  directed 
 towards  continuing  the  building  a  new  community  leisure  centre  in  Kingston  town 
 centre,  Decarbonisation  schemes  essential  for  achieving  net  zero,  and  the  new 
 Post-16  Campus,  which  will  equip  young  people  with  Special  Educational  Needs 
 and Disabilities (SEND) with work-ready skills within the local community. 
 163.  The  Council  also  continues  the  general  fund  rolling  programmes  of  investment 
 street  lighting,  road  maintenance  and  works  to  operational  properties,  with  a  total 
 of  £4.5m  per  year.  The  Technology  Investment  Fund  has  been  removed  from  the 
 capital  programme  and  replaced  by  a  specific  Digital  Transformation  programme, 
 funded by capital receipts. 
 164.  The  assumed  level  of  prudential  borrowing  to  finance  the  General  Fund  capital 
 programme  is  £102.85m,  as  detailed  in  Annex  11.  The  majority  of  which  will  be 
 spent  on  the  new  community  leisure  centre  and  financing  of  initial  phases  of  the 
 CRE  programme.  The  profile  of  the  funding  requirement  is  dependent  on  forecast 
 income such as GLA funding and capital receipts.

 investing  in  its  housing  stock  of  rented  and  leaseholder  properties.  Each  year  the

 169.  Project  delays  and  other  market  factors  have  resulted  in  a  significant  in-year 
 forecast  underspend  of  the  capital  programme.  The  last  forecast  presented  in  the 
 public  domain  was  an  underspend  of  £5.4m  as  at  month  6  (September  2025).  A 
 further  update  on  the  in-year  position  was  reported  to  the  Corporate  and 
 Resources  Committee  in  February  2026.  Further  work  will  be  undertaken  to 
 ensure  capital  project  forecasts  are  profiled  suitably  across  the  years,  taking  into 
 account  any  potential  delays  arising  at  various  stages  within  the  project,  to  support 
 with the financing and minimising the costs of borrowing for the Authority. 
 170.  In  the  initial  stages  of  development,  major  capital  projects  will  have  significant 
 uncertainties.  For  example  these  may  relate  to  the  planning  process,  the  views  / 
 interest  of  stakeholders  who  must  be  consulted,  ground  conditions,  or  the  cost  of 
 refurbishing  or  demolishing  existing  buildings  (e.g.  the  cost  of  asbestos  removal). 
 As  such  all  major  projects  contain  a  defined  contingency  provision.  The  level  of 
 this  contingency  is  set  with  reference  to  the  type  of  project,  the  complexity  and  risk 
 items identified on the risk register.

 CIPFA Financial Management Code and Resilience Index 
 176.  The  CIPFA  Financial  Management  Code  (“The  Code”)  is  designed  to  support  good 
 practice  in  financial  management  and  to  assist  local  authorities  in  demonstrating 
 their  financial  sustainability.  Although  the  Code  does  not  have  legislative  backing, 
 it  is  applicable  to  all  Local  Authorities.  It  is  consistent  with  other  codes  and 
 guidance  including  the  CIPFA  Prudential  Code,  the  Treasury  Management  in  the 
 Public  Sector  Code  of  Practice  and  the  Code  of  Practice  on  Local  Authority 
 Accounting in the UK. 
 177.  The  existing  financial  management  arrangements  in  place  during  2025/26  have 
 been  reviewed  against  the  standards  set  out  across  seven  thematic  sections  in  the 
 Code from last year. 
 178.  Overall  the  Council  continues  to  demonstrate  a  strong  level  of  compliance  with  the 
 Code. 
 179.  Further  to  the  Financial  Management  Code,  CIPFA  publishes  each  year  the  CIPFA 
 Resilience  index  which  shows  the  Council's  position  on  a  range  of  measures 
 associated  with  financial  risk  highlighting  where  additional  scrutiny  may  be 
 required. 
 180.  The  data  for  the  resilience  index  is  obtained  from  the  Revenue  Expenditure  and 
 Financing  England  Outturn  Report  2024-25  ('RO  Forms')  and  reflects  figures 
 submitted  by  Local  Authorities  to  MHCLG,  published  on  4  December  2025.  A 
 provisional index has been released to S151 Officers to review.
