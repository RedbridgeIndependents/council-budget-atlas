4. Introduction and Background 
 
Council Tax 
 
Maximum Increases and Referendum Principles 
 
4.1 The ability to raise council tax before a referendum is 2.99% in 2026/27, the 
same as in 2025/26. In addition, authorities can levy an Adult social care 
precept of 2% in 2026/27, the same level as in 2025/26. 
 
 Funding Framework 
 
4.2 A fundamental review of local authority financing was carried out during 2025 
which now includes multiyear settlements from 2026/27 for 3 years. The 
underlying assumption for Council Tax within Local government funding is 
that council tax is expected to increase by 4.99% per annum during this 
period. The proposed increase in council tax this year provides an 
opportunity to build upon the investments made by Greenwich and build 
some financial resilience into services. 
 
Borough Element of Council Tax 
 
4.3 The period since 2008/09 has seen long periods of freezes in the borough 
element of council tax, during times of low inflation.  
 
4.4 In 2025/26 the majority of London boroughs raised the borough element of 
council tax by either the maximum amount or close to it (i.e. 2.99%). 
 
4.5 In terms of the overall direction of travel for council tax across London, 
Greenwich (Table 3) remains in a relatively good position:  
 
• 11th lowest borough element of council tax in 2025/26 (Appendix A1) 
• 17th lowest rise for the period 2008/09 to 2025/26 (Appendix A2)

Robustness of the Budget Estimates  
  
6.3 The MTFS is a continuous process and the journey ending with the 
production of this report was started almost immediately upon completion of 
the equivalent report in 2025. Estimates are made at various points along 
the journey and these are reviewed and tested as time moves on to ensure 
they are still valid. They are still estimates and / or evidence of a plan that 
exists at a point in time, but which may or may not come to fruition for a 
variety of factors and could be within or outside of the council’s influence or 
control. These estimates cannot give guaranteed assurances, but give 
Members reasonable assurances that the budget is set on the best available 
information, evidence and data.  
  
6.4 The process for developing the MTFS starts with the estimated gap set out in 
the latest report (usually the budget report) and updated from there for 
economic, demand, price and other relevant factors, in consultation with 
departments.  In this context the budget presented for approval 
includes estimates about inflation, contractual uplifts, demand 
growth, additional income, fees and charges, changes to Government funding 
streams and potential savings and efficiencies.  
  
6.5 Whilst the Director of Resources ensures that the overall process drives 
forward, it is a collaborative effort including, the Lead Member for Finance, 
Resources and Social Value, the Leader, the Chief Executive, members of 
Cabinet, the Greenwich Management Team, senior officers and heads of 
service. Scrutiny provides pre decision review of the 
proposals.  Each initial case for a proposal was taken through a process, which 
included numerous Greenwich Strategic Leadership Team (Cabinet and 
Greenwich Management Team) meetings, where members and officers 
worked together to challenge and develop the ideas.

Reserves  
  
6.13 Reserves are one off in nature and once used, they are gone. Reserves 
are generally held for three reasons:  
  
• a working balance to help cushion the impact of uneven cash 
flows and avoid unnecessary temporary borrowing – this forms part 
of general reserves  
• a contingency to cushion the impact of unexpected events or 
emergencies – this also forms part of general reserves  
• a means of building up funds, often referred to as earmarked 
reserves, to meet known or predicted requirements.  
  
6.14 There is no statutory definition of a minimum level of reserves, and it is for 
this reason that the matter falls to the judgement of the Section 151 Officer. 
In coming to a judgement on this matter the Section 151 Officer has 
considered the following matters:   
  
• Risks inherent in the budget strategy.   
• Risk management policies and strategies   
• Past financial performance against budget   
• Current budget projections   
• The robustness of estimates contained within the budget.   
• The adequacy of financial controls and budget monitoring 
procedures   
• Spending pressures   
• Increases in Social Care Precept and council tax.   
• Impact of cost of living, energy prices and inflation and 
other market pressures   
• Impact of the economic downturn on council tax and 
business rates    
• Capital decisions and implementation
