1. Business and Financial Planning 2026/27 to 2028/2029 
 
(a) This report brought together the Council’s business and financial planning and 
looks forward over the next three years to set out how it will support the 
Council’s objectives under the Fairer Westminster strategy, supported by a 
medium-term financial plan and balanced budget for 2026/27. 
 
(b) This report outlines a balanced budget for 2026/27 that includes a proposed 
2% increase in council tax and a one-off use of reserves of £19.7m. The 
medium-term financial plan forecasts an ongoing budget gap of £87.8m by the 
third year. Central government funding for 2026/27 is confirmed and indicative 
grant figures are provided through the multi-year settlement for 2027/28 and 
2028/29. 
 
(c) The proposed 2026/27 revenue budget assumes a freeze in the general 
council tax element and a 2% increase for the adult’s social care precept 
element. At Band D this will result in an annual increase of £10.54 or an 
equivalent weekly amount of 20p per week. When combined, the general 
element of council tax and adults social care precept, for Westminster, will rise 
from £526.80 to £537.34 at Band D. 
 
(d) The proposed budget reflects the significant reduction in grant funding that the 
Council will be impacted by through the changes that have been made as part 
of the Fair Funding Review. The reductions are phased over the next three 
years, with £75.6m of annual funding coming out by the third year. Floor 
protection funding of £24.9m remains in the third year. 
 


=== PDF PAGE 2 ===
(e) In light of the major changes to grant funding the Council has scoped out a 
whole council transformation programme which will ensure the organisation 
can maintain services and deliver them while remaining modern, people-
centred and financially resilient. This programme requires investment in the 
short term but can deliver substantial efficiencies and a minimum forecast of 
£30m is included. 
 
(f) The recommended net General Fund budget for 2026/27 is £282.666m, this is 
a net decrease of £11.4m on the 2025/26 adjusted net budget. The budget 
changes include costs pressures and investments totalling £56.4m including; 
further Fairer Westminster investments of £2.1m, additional resources for 
temporary accommodation and Adults Social Care pressures of £9.3m, other 
service cost pressures including inflation of £27.6m, increased capital 
financing costs of £3.5m, decreased interest earnings of £6m, transformation 
investment of £2.5m and other changes of £5.3m. 
 
(g) Savings and cost reductions totalling £54.5m including new savings of 
£27.8m, transformation savings of £3m, previously agreed savings proposals 
of £12.7m, reduced employer pension contributions of £8m and council tax 
collection fund surplus of £3m. Fair Funding grant reductions including HPG 
grant loss totalling £26.4m. The balance is then funded from increased council 
tax income including second homes premium of £8.6m and £19.7m from 
reserves and balances.

2. Capital Strategy 2026/27 to 2030/31, Forecast Position for 
2025/26 and Future Years Forecast to 2039/40 
 
(a) The report set out the Council’s five-year capital strategy from 2026/27 to 
2030/31 and summarises the position up to 2039/40. 
 
(b) The general fund capital programme (as detailed in Appendix A) proposes a 
gross budget of £2.550bn and a net budget of £1.275bn (after taking into 
account income from grants and capital receipts). The capital programme of 
the Housing Revenue Account (HRA) is set out separately in the HRA 
Business Plan which accompanies this report as part of the Council’s annual 
budget setting process. 
 
(c) The long-term capital investment plan is underpinned by the Council’s Fairer 
Westminster policy objectives. Capital proposals are considered within the 
Council’s overall medium to long-term priorities, and the preparation of the 
capital programme is an integral part of the financial planning process. This 
includes taking account of the revenue implications of the projects as part of 
the revenue budget setting process. 
 
(d) The overall capital programme this year has increased with significant 
additional proposed investment in areas such as temporary accommodation 
acquisitions, this is offset by significant revenue savings in the future. The 
Council continues to set aside additional revenue funding each year to cover 
the financing costs of the programme, this is reflected in the proposed 
revenue budget. Earmarked reserves are also maintained to support the 
financing of the capital programme and provide a sustainable approach to 
funding through the Medium-Term Financial Plan.

the Act require the Council to have regard to the Prudential Code for Capital 
Finance in Local Authorities and to set Prudential Indicators for the next three 
years to ensure that the Council’s capital investment plans are affordable, 
prudent and sustainable. These are contained within the report we considered 
and attached at Appendix 4.

(b) The Act also requires the Council to set out a statement of its treasury 
management strategy for borrowing and to prepare an Annual Investment 
Strategy (as shown in Appendix 1). This sets out the Council’s policies for 
managing its investments and for giving priority to the security and liquidity of 
those investments. The Treasury Management Strategy Statement (TMSS) 
and Annual Investment Strategy (AIS) must both have regard to guidance 
issued by the Ministry for Housing, Communities and Local Government 
(MHCLG) and must be agreed by Full Council.

TMSS) and AIS, and related reports during the financial year.

5. Housing Revenue Account Business Plan 
 
(a) This report presented the updated 30-year Housing Revenue Account (HRA) 
Business Plan which the Council is required to prepare on an annual basis. It 
provided an overview of the financial planning that supports the management 
of the 20,400 homes operated by the Council’s HRA. This covers both 

=== PDF PAGE 8 ===
revenue and capital expenditure and therefore incorporates the extensive 
Housing Investment Plan. 
 
(b) Despite an economic and legislative backdrop that continues to present the 
HRA with significant financial challenges, the Council has delivered a 
sustainable HRA Business Plan from 2026/27 onwards. In doing so, it has 
managed to safeguard all planned housing investment maintaining the 
Council’s Fairer Westminster vision and the aim to deliver more social 
housing within Westminster. 
 
(c) Housing is a significant pillar within this vision and the HRA Business Plan is 
therefore a critical enabler for Council in delivering key strategic outcomes. As 
well as maximising the delivery of social housing, the plan supports 
investment in several other important objectives, including improving the 
condition and safety of existing stock, investing in retrofit schemes for our 
homes and replacing the Pimlico District Heating Undertaking (PDHU) 
network. 
 
(d) The report seeks approval for the HRA revenue budget for 2026/27. This 
includes approval for the proposed uplifts to both HRA rents and non-dwelling 
charges to take effect from 1st April 2026. The Council is proposing to take 
advantage of the 4.8% rent increase for 2026/27 (in line with the rents 
standard set by the national rent policy). The Council also intends to increase 
non-dwelling rents by 3.8% in line with CPI as per its wider fee & charges 
policy. It is aware of the pressure on household incomes and proposes to 
extend the Rent Support Fund to provide targeted support to tenants 
earmarking a further £1.000m for this purpose. Based on these proposals, the 
HRA will generate £159.3m across a range of income streams in 2026/27 (of 
which £105.9m, or 66%, comes from tenant rents). The 2026/27 expenditure 
budget is £159.2m.  
 
(e) The report also seeks approval for the 5-year HRA capital programme which 
includes anticipated capital expenditure of £931.1m. The 30-year programme 
totals £2.5bn. Approximately 40% of the programme is funded from external 
sources (£1.1bn) with the remainder supported by the HRA itself. This 
includes a requirement to borrow an additional £860.3m over the next 15 
years. The business plan ensures this is prudent by setting minimum reserve 
levels, applying a minimum interest cover ratio, and creating appropriate 
contingencies to manage risk.
