3. 
 Reasons for Recommendations  
 
3.1 
 The Council must set its revenue budget and Council Tax by 11 March of the 
preceding financial year. If, for whatever reason, the Council cannot agree a budget 
and Council Tax at Council on 5 march, Members should be aware that it is unlikely 
that the Coun
cil Tax bills could be sent out on time for the 1 April instalment (taking 
into account the necessary notice period and the time required to print the bills 
and accompanying leaflet).  
 
3.2 
 It is also good practice to approve the capital investment programme, treasury 
management strategy and fees & charges at the same time as the revenue budget. 
These are included within this report, together with their implications for the 
2026/27 revenue bu
dget and the Medium Term Financial Strategy (MTFS).  
 
3.3 
 The Housing Revenue Account (HRA) budget also needs to be agreed in a timely 
manner to ensure rent levels can be reflected from 1 April of each year. A separate 
report elsewhere on this agenda deals with Housing Rents, Charges and the HRA 
budget for 2026/2

4. 
 Background  
 
4.1 
 The 
Executive Director of Resources, as the statutory Chief Finance Officer, is required 
by statute to advise Members on the level of reserves and robustness of estimates as 
part of the annual budget
 -
setting report.  This assessment is summarised in 
paragraph 
13.3 of this report and set out in detail in the Cabinet report of 26 February 
2026 in Appendix A. The Budget report also includes the required recommendations 
on prudential indicators for the Council to approve. The significant budget risks 
facing the Cou
 ncil are set out in Appendix A Section 9.   
 
4.2  
The significant risks identified include:  
 
• 
 Delivery of the transformational change required to deliver a Recovery Plan to 
allow the authority to not rely on EFS beyond 2028/29  
• 
 Capacity and ability to deliver savings to value and profile  
• 
 Funding volatility: Although provisional settlement figures are positive, only 
2026/27 funding is confirmed; the figure for 2027/28 and 2028/29 remain 
indicative and may be subject to change following future fiscal events, distribution 
reviews or economic 
 adjustments. 
• 
 2029/30 remains a cliff edge for most local authorities and there is no visibility of 
funding plans beyond the current three year settlement period  
• 
 Increasing costs due to inflation and demographic pressures resulting in 
significant savings requirements in future years  
• 
 Adult Social Care, Childrens Social Care and Homelessness which potentially 
continue to cause pressures on the Council’s revenue budget  
• 
 The expectation that the mitigation and cost reduction plans implicit in decisions 
taken about the amount of pressures rolling forward from 2025/26 reflected in 
service budget growth are successful. This particularly relates to homelessness and 
adult socia
l care. 
• 
 Inherited liabilities for now aging infrastructure for which the council is 
inadequately resourced  
• 
 The increased reliance on parking and highway contravention income to 
contribute to the costs of transportation services  
• 
 The cumulative impact of savings plans and the increasing challenges of achieving 
the on
 -
going significant budget reductions of organisational transformation to 
maintain service provision

7. 
 Fairness Implications, including Equality and Diversity  
 
General  
 
7.1 
 In summary, section 149 of the 2010 Act requires the Council, when exercising its 
functions, to have ‘due regard’ to the need to:  
 
a) 
 Eliminate discrimination, harassment and victimisation and any other conduct 
that is prohibited by or under the Act (which includes conduct prohibited 
under section 29); 
 
b) 
 Advance equality of opportunity between people who share a relevant 
protected characteristic and those who don’t share it;  
 
c) 
 Foster good relations between people who share a relevant protected 
characteristic and those who do not (which involves having due regard, in 
particular, to the need to tackle prejudice and promote understanding).  
  
7.2 
 Under the PSED the relevant protected characteristics are:

13. 
 Legal Implications  
 
13.1 
 The Local Government Act 2000 requires full Council to approve the Council’s budget 
and council tax demand. Management of budgets, the power to invest and ability to 
borrow are contained in the Local Government Act 2003.  
 
13.2 
 Section 25 of the Local Government Act 2003 requires that when a local authority is 
agreeing its budget and precept, the Chief Finance Officer must report to it on the 
following matters: (a) the robustness of the estimates made for the purposes of the 
calc
ulations, and (b) the adequacy of the proposed financial reserves. The authority 
must have due regard to the report when making decisions on the budget and 
precept. 
 

=== PDF PAGE 13 ===
13.3 
 Section 31 of the Local Government Finance Act 1992 imposes a statutory duty upon 
the authority to calculate its budget requirements for each financial year. There is a 
complex statutory framework which prescribes how the budget requirement must 
be calcula
ted and this will be set out in more detail in the report to Budget Council in 
February when Members are asked to agree the budget and Council Tax.  
 
13.4 
 A local authority is not permitted to allow its spending to exceed its available 
resources so that overall it would be in deficit (Sections 32 and 43 of the Local 
Government Finance Act 1992 require authorities to have regard to the level of 
reserves neede
 d for meeting estimated future expenditure when calculating the 
budget requirement).  
 
13.5 
 Under Section 31A of the Local Government Finance Act 1992, budget calculations 
have to be made before 11 March, but they are not invalid merely because they are 
made on or after 11 March. However, delay in setting the Council Tax will have very 
serious fi
 nancial consequences. It will render the Council vulnerable to legal 
proceedings requiring it to set the tax. In any event, it is important that the tax is set 
well in advance of 1 April as no sum is payable for Council Tax until 14 days after the 
date of 
posting bills. Serious financial losses will accrue very soon from a late setting 
of Council Tax as income is delayed and interest is foregone. An important feature of 
Council Tax is that the statutory budget calculation must be followed exactly. If not 
th
e Council Tax resolution will be invalid and void.  
 
13.6 
 Any proposals which have been considered as part of the budget making process and 
which, if implemented, would result in the significant change or reduction in services 
will be subject to appropriate consultation with service users and other stakeholders 
s
o that the full extent of the impact of such proposals can be assessed prior to making 
a final decision on whether to implement the proposal.  
 
13.7 
 Certain proposals which may result in redundancies or changes to terms and 
conditions of staff may also be subject to statutory consultation under employment 
legislation. Decisions on changes to staffing structure or terms will be decided in 
accordance wit
 h the Constitution and delegated powers where applicable.  
 
13.8 
 The public sector equality duty as referenced in the body of the report will also need 
to be considered at various stages throughout the budget setting process. This has 
been done by undertaking equality impact assessments at appropriate stages which 
ident
ify for the decision maker any consequential adverse impacts on protected 
groups and should assist them in also developing appropriate mitigations where 
necessary. 
 
13.9 
 The obligation to make a lawful budget each year is shared equally by each individual 
Member. In discharging that obligation, Members owe a fiduciary duty to the Council 
Taxpayer.   
 
13.10 
 The budget must not include expenditure on items which would fall outside the 
Council’s powers.  Expenditure on lawful items must be prudent, and any forecasts or 
assumptions such as rates of interest or inflation must themselves be rational.  Power 
to spe
 nd money must be exercised bona fide for the purpose for which they were 
conferred and any ulterior motives risk a finding of illegality. In determining the 
Council’s overall budget requirement, Members are bound to have regard to the level 
of Council Tax 
necessary to sustain it.  Essentially, the interests of the Council Taxpayer 
must be balanced against those of the various service recipients.

within the 2026/27 financial year, especially on the part of the Cabinet. Setting a

budget

provision for such expenditure. However, Members will bear in mind that in making
