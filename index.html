Schools and learning

A school budget is not every school.

Education in the council return covers the provision funded through the council. Centrally funded academies sit outside this boundary. Different mixes of maintained schools and academies can change the comparison without a change in the needs of children.[5][6]

The chart now opens the education total into its six published components. A pupil-normalised comparison would need pupil counts matched to those funding responsibilities. That denominator has not been imported.

Care and support

Population gives scale. Not need.

Dividing by residents makes councils of different sizes easier to put alongside one another. It does not adjust for disability, deprivation, care intensity or local prices. Adult social care in this return excludes expenditure funded by NHS transfers.[1][5]

This lens shows the eight themes per resident, using ONS mid-2025 estimates. It is a scale comparison, not an efficiency score or a 2026–27 population forecast.[8]

04 / The denominator test

What does a mile
actually buy?

The right numerator matters as much as the denominator. Do not divide the entire transport budget by road length and call it road maintenance.

Start with structural maintenance

RA line 230 is the planned net revenue amount for structural maintenance. The network comparison below uses DfT’s 2025 major locally managed roads + all minor roads. The source is already in miles; no kilometre conversion is applied.[1][7]

Redbridge
£1,718,000 planned structural maintenance ÷ (36 + 295) miles = £5,190 per network mile[1][7]

This is a network-scale proxy, not an audited cost per maintained mile. DfT lengths are rounded; network scope can include roads managed by another London body, and revenue maintenance excludes capital works. Road condition, traffic and treatment area are not held constant. A lower ratio is not necessarily better.[5][7]
