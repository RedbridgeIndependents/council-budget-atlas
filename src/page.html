Tax, grants and rates are not the same thing

The council’s own council-tax requirement is RA line 990. Billing councils also collect precepts for bodies such as the GLA, police and fire; those other bodies have their own returns. This atlas does not treat the whole bill as the borough’s funding. The collection-fund adjustment is shown separately, not added to council tax without explanation.[1][5]

Central-government grants here mean specific/special grants inside AEF + Revenue Support Grant + police grant, from explicit financing lines. Retained business rates are a separate source. Dedicated Schools Grant is identified as part of specific grants, not counted twice or assumed to be freely spendable.[1][3]

A reconciliation, not a residual

The funding mix below reconciles to revenue expenditure (RA 900), not to the different net service total (RA 799). Funding contributions reverse the sign of the return’s credits; own council tax retains its reported sign. Negative reserve contributions mean planned additions to reserves, not a new government grant.

Shares use the explicitly reconciling revenue-expenditure total as denominator, so a negative financing contribution produces a negative share. No percentage is calculated when the total is zero or unavailable. Gross service income and grants outside AEF are not appended to this already-net perimeter.[1][5]

Actual council-tax receipts and collection rates are not imported here. They belong to separate collection statistics and years, not the 2026–27 budget mix. Schools and learning

A school budget is not every school.

Education in the council return covers the provision funded through the council. Centrally funded academies sit outside this boundary. Different mixes of maintained schools and academies can change the comparison without a change in the needs of children.[5][6]

The chart now opens the education total into its six published components. A pupil-normalised comparison would need pupil counts matched to those funding responsibilities. That denominator has not been imported.

Care and support

Population gives scale. Not need.

Dividing by residents makes councils of different sizes easier to put alongside one another. It does not adjust for disability, deprivation, care intensity or local prices. Adult social care in this return excludes expenditure funded by NHS transfers.[1][5]

This lens shows the eight themes per resident, using ONS mid-2025 estimates. It is a scale comparison, not an efficiency score or a 2026–27 population forecast.[8]

04 / The denominator test

What does a mile
actually buy?

The right numerator matters as much as the denominator. Do not divide the entire transport budget by road length and call it road maintenance.

Start with structural maintenance

RA line 230 is the planned net revenue amount for structural maintenance. The network comparison below uses DfT’s 2025 major locally managed roads + all minor roads. The source is already in miles; no kilometre conversion is applied.[1][7]

This is a network-scale proxy, not an audited cost per maintained mile. DfT lengths are rounded; network scope can include roads managed by another London body, and revenue maintenance excludes capital works. Road condition, traffic and treatment area are not held constant. A lower ratio is not necessarily better.[5][7]

Keep the gaps visible

The same denominator can provide cautious network-scale context for environmental/routine maintenance and winter service. It is intentionally unavailable for transport planning, concessionary fares, parking, street lighting and all-transport totals. A lighting-column count would be a better unit for street lighting.

07 / The small print is part of the story

Comparable does
not mean identical.

Included

England

415 return authorities; 406 returned. This includes 317 councils and 98 other authorities. All are searchable, but police, fire, parks, waste and combined authorities are not borough peers. London has 33 council entries including the City: 32 returns, or 31 of the 32 boroughs once the City is excluded.[1][4]

Not harmonised

Scotland

Scottish Government publishes its own local-government finance series. It is linked here, not merged into England’s RA categories. Service responsibilities, grant treatment and definitions need a documented crosswalk before a UK-wide league table is defensible.[10]

Not harmonised

Wales

Welsh Government’s 2026–27 revenue budget and capital forecast is a separate publication, released 18 June 2026. Its service classification and totals have not been mapped into this edition. Missing harmonisation is not zero expenditure.[11]

Different duties

Northern Ireland

Councils do not deliver the same services as London boroughs: education, personal social services, roads and public housing are outside their responsibilities. No Northern Ireland budget is imported or ranked against English boroughs.[12]

What the money means

How services are consolidated

Common official codes, not similar-sounding names. Every harmonised comparison uses the same 2026–27 England RA/SG return part, asset code, line and definition. The 321 canonical fields retain their original labels, source columns and council-specific source rows. Shorter editorial theme labels are display aliases only: they do not merge money or change scope.[1][2][3][6]

The 33 local report records retain the council’s own label, year, scope, amount and page locator where transcribed. No local headline has been verified as an equivalent RA service breakdown. All remain explicitly unmapped and excluded from service benchmarks; no directorate is allocated to narrower services by name similarity. Transport is not road maintenance; children’s services are not automatically children’s social care; General Fund housing is not HRA. Missing breakdowns are gaps, not inferred splits.

Only the 13 mutually exclusive published theme totals are added to RA 799, checked for every submitted return. Parents are never added to their components. No unverified many-to-one aggregation or one-to-many allocation is performed. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have no established crosswalk here.

Inspect canonical service codes, original labels and aliases

Service lines: nominal 2026–27 budget amounts, originally in £ thousands, net of service sales, fees, charges and specified transfers. Values are multiplied by 1,000 before any per-unit division. No inflation-adjusted time series is constructed.[1]

Gross spending: not available for every service in this release. A net service budget must not be described as gross expenditure. HRA income and expenditure are separately labelled in part 2.[2]

Grants and financing: grants are not extra service spending to add to the service total. The SG workbook includes Dedicated Schools Grant and grants inside/outside Aggregate External Finance; they are shown as financing lines, not piled on top of education. “Capital items” in RA part 2 are accounting items, not a complete capital investment programme.[2][3][5]

Missing is not zero

Reported numeric zero, a missing return ([x]), not applicable and suppressed values have distinct states. This vintage contains [x] values for the nine absent returns. No service-level applicability is inferred from a zero; responsibility differences remain visible.

The non-returning authorities are . Their rows remain present, with no invented budget values. Official England totals are grossed estimates, not a sum of only the submitted council rows.[4][5]

Boundaries and reorganisations

The 2026–27 RA roster is preserved as published, by ONS and return identifier. Former councils are not carried forward, new authorities are not added to their predecessors, and overlapping counties, districts and special authorities are never summed as “England councils”. All 415 entries and their responsibility classes can be inspected in the explorer.

Exact-code joins only: ONS population has no matching code for the RA entries for and . Their per-resident comparisons stay unavailable; names are not used as a fuzzy boundary crosswalk. The denominator audit below enumerates every unjoined authority.[1][8]

Denominator vintage and limits

Residents: ONS mid-2025, MYE2 Persons, all ages (published 1 October 2026). Joined by exact ONS area code. Per-resident values describe population scale only, not demand, need, efficiency or quality. [8][9]

Roads: DfT RDL0102a, 2025, updated 12 February 2026; locally managed major roads plus minor roads in miles, using source-rounded components. No denominator is borrowed from a county for a district.[7]

Other service units: pupil/place counts, care users, households assisted, tonnes of waste, planning cases and other units are catalogued but not imported. Their official collections are linked as routes to future matched datasets, not cited as measured denominators in this edition.[15][16][17][18][19]

What was actually checked

The importer preserves every numeric field in the two RA parts and the SG return. For every submitted authority, the 13 theme totals reconcile exactly to total service expenditure. The England theme totals are checked against the separate national Table 2 to source rounding.

These are internal and publisher consistency checks, not three independent confirmations of every cell. The London report ledger adds primary council documents, but different accounting scopes mean not every local headline is reconciled to the RA return. No claim of full triangulation is made. Council reports, government releases and republished tables may share the same underlying return.

All 24 candidate local-report headlines were rechecked against preserved PDFs: 23 are supported; Harrow’s £215m was withheld because the source described 2025–26, not 2026–27. The report ledger explains each retained amount’s year, units, scope and page; none is substituted into a service benchmark. Bromley’s adopted-year public booklet was retrieved, but its full technical budget report remained inaccessible.

Reproducible checks compare all 133,215 imported monetary cells with their preserved workbook coordinates, recompute every authority/field conversion and all supported benchmark lenses, and verify financing, peer exclusions, medians, signed differences, denominator joins and missing states. This is one 2026–27 source snapshot, not an audit of the underlying official estimates. Unreconciled local headlines never enter conclusions.

Display precision: £m amounts use three decimal places (the source’s £ thousands); resident/network ratios round to the nearest £ and funding/difference percentages to one decimal. Calculations, ordering and CSV values use unrounded inputs. No percentages are shown for zero/missing financing totals or zero/negative spending benchmarks; a net-income benchmark still has a valid signed cash difference. Rounding means displayed financing shares need not sum to exactly 100.0%.

Visual evidence, not decoration

Common scales, direct labels, accessible tables and explicit missingness follow principles associated with Edward Tufte’s The Visual Display of Quantitative Information and Beautiful Evidence, and Stephen Few’s Information Dashboard Design. The latter is by Few, not Tufte. No book text is reproduced.[13][14]

Council identities

Official logo/brand swatches sit beside readable text in both themes; text is never forced into an inaccessible brand colour. Redbridge’s requested dark red is its official primary-palette chestnut, not the black wordmark or a claim that its current pink header is dark red. Solid versus outlined bars and direct names remain available when identities share a colour. An empty dashed swatch means the identity colour has not been verified.
